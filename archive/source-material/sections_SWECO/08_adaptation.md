# Adaptation Measures

This section presents climate adaptation measures to address the risks identified in Sections [X.6]–[X.7]. Measures are organised as: (i) general cross-cutting provisions applicable to the full project; and (ii) component-specific measures addressing the highest-rated hazard–component combinations. Where adaptation measures are already embedded in the current project design, this is noted. Where additional measures are recommended beyond the current scope, these are clearly identified.

## General Adaptation Measures

The following cross-cutting measures apply to the project as a whole and address the overarching direction of climate change in the project area: progressive warming, increasing moisture deficit, and growing water demand under climate stress.

**Climate-adjusted design standards.** All new construction and reconstruction components should be designed using climate-adjusted temperature, precipitation, and flow parameters derived from the projections in Section [X.4], rather than historical observed norms. Design standards for drainage capacity, flood protection levels, pipe burial depths, and thermal performance of equipment should incorporate the projected climate conditions for the mid-to-end-of-design-life horizon (2050–2075), particularly under the SSP2-4.5 and SSP3-7.0 scenarios. This applies to civil, mechanical, and electrical specifications.

**Non-revenue water (NRW) reduction.** The rehabilitation of transmission pipelines (Baku I & II, OGB) and the construction of the Saray–Sharg pipeline are directly aligned with NRW reduction objectives. Under increasing climate stress — particularly drought periods when source water availability decreases — every cubic metre lost to leakage represents an amplified loss in effective supply capacity. The project's NRW interventions therefore represent a primary climate adaptation measure by maximising the yield from existing sources. This measure is already incorporated in the project design.

**Water source diversification and system integration.** The project improves the integration and operational flexibility of the Greater Baku water supply network, allowing operators to vary the balance of supply from the OGB pipeline (groundwater), Baku I & II (groundwater), and the Samur–Absheron Canal (surface water) in response to source availability conditions. Groundwater sources (OGB, Baku I & II) are generally more buffered against short-term drought than surface water sources (Canal, Jeyranbatan Reservoir) and should be prioritised during drought periods. The Saray–Sharg pipeline enhances the network's ability to route water between reservoirs during source-specific deficits. This measure is embedded in the project's design logic.

**Climate monitoring and operational adaptation protocols.** AZERSU should establish or strengthen real-time monitoring of key climate-sensitive system variables, including: Samur River and Tahtakorpu Reservoir water levels and inflows; Jeyranbatan Reservoir storage volume; raw water temperature and quality at WTP intake; and heat index forecasts for operational planning. These monitoring systems should be linked to pre-defined drought and heat-wave response protocols, specifying operational actions (source switching, demand management, reservoir drawdown rules) at defined trigger thresholds. Such early-warning protocols are a low-cost, high-value adaptation measure that can significantly reduce the operational impact of extreme climate events.

**Climate change mainstreaming in O&M planning.** Long-term operations and maintenance (O&M) plans for all project components should explicitly incorporate the projected climate change trajectory, including regular review of risk ratings as new climate projections become available. Periodic inspection programmes for pipelines and canal sections should be intensified for components and segments identified as high-risk in Table [X].12.

## Component-Specific Adaptation Measures

### Jeyranbatan Water Treatment Plant

The primary adaptation priorities for the WTP are managing the impacts of increasing heat on treatment processes and worker safety, and ensuring treatment capacity remains adequate as source water quality is affected by drought-driven reservoir stress.

**Heat-resilient design of the reconstructed plant.** The reconstruction of the conventional treatment units should incorporate heat-resilient design standards: (i) equipment specifications that account for elevated ambient temperatures projected for 2050 and beyond (mean summer temperatures +1.5–2.0°C above current baseline under SSP2-4.5); (ii) enhanced cooling provisions for electrical rooms, control systems, and chemical dosing equipment; (iii) where technically feasible, enclosure or shading of outdoor process units to reduce thermal loading; and (iv) design of membrane systems with consideration of elevated feed water temperatures, which affect flux and fouling rates.

**Enhanced treatment capacity for drought-affected raw water.** The design of the reconstructed plant should incorporate flexibility to handle increased raw water turbidity, algal loading, and dissolved organic matter concentrations associated with low-level drought conditions in the Jeyranbatan Reservoir (warmer water temperatures, longer hydraulic residence times). This may include enhanced coagulation flexibility, additional pre-treatment capacity, and more frequent filter cleaning cycles as design basis assumptions.

**Site drainage and flood protection.** The WTP site layout and drainage design should be reviewed against the High river flood hazard classification for Baku/Absheron and updated intensity-frequency-duration parameters for extreme rainfall. Chemical storage and containment areas should be flood-proofed to current bunded containment standards.

**Intake screening for dust and wind-blown particulates.** Intake screening at the WTP should be designed or upgraded to minimise ingress of wind-blown dust and sand, consistent with the known Khazri wind conditions on the Absheron Peninsula. This is a relatively low-cost measure with direct operational benefit in reducing maintenance frequency.

### Baku I and Baku II Transmission Pipelines

The primary adaptation priorities are managing landslide risk in the Guba-Khachmaz piedmont zone and ensuring river crossing integrity against flood and scour.

**Landslide risk assessment for at-risk sections.** A targeted geotechnical survey of pipeline sections in the Greater Caucasus piedmont zone (Guba-Khachmaz) should be conducted as part of pre-rehabilitation planning to identify segments at elevated landslide risk. Findings should prioritise rehabilitation sequencing and inform protective measures (slope stabilisation, pipeline re-routing where required, enhanced burial, or protective covering).

**River crossing inspection and protection.** All river crossings along the Baku I and II pipeline routes should be inspected for scour, bank erosion, and deterioration of crossing support structures. Crossings identified as at risk should be protected or rehabilitated as a priority, incorporating design flood levels consistent with climate-adjusted flow estimates. Where crossings are vulnerable to significant scour, directional drilling (below the active scour zone) may be considered as part of the rehabilitation programme.

**Monitoring programme for high-risk segments.** A pipeline integrity monitoring programme should be established, prioritising sections identified as at elevated risk from landslide and flooding. This could incorporate periodic ground survey, satellite-based ground movement detection, and acoustic leak detection.

### Saray–Sharg Main Pipeline

As a new-construction project, the Saray–Sharg pipeline offers the greatest opportunity to incorporate climate resilience at minimal incremental cost through design from first principles.

**Climate-adjusted route selection and design.** The pipeline route and alignment should be selected to avoid flood-prone corridors and areas of mapped ground instability. Burial depth should be specified to account for both the thermal environment (avoiding temperature-sensitive pipe behaviour) and the local scour risk at any watercourse crossings. Design flood levels at crossings should be based on climate-projected peak flows for the 2075 horizon. Pipe materials and joint systems should be selected for durability under projected temperature ranges.

**Seismic design provisions.** The pipeline should be designed to appropriate seismic standards for the Absheron zone (Medium hazard), including flexible joint systems at locations of anticipated differential ground movement.

### Oghuz–Gabala–Baku Pipeline

The primary adaptation priorities are addressing river flooding and earthquake risk along the Aran zone pipeline corridor, which represent the highest combined hazard severity in the project.

**Flood protection at Kura-Araz river crossings.** Priority should be given to inspection and rehabilitation of all river crossings in the Aran lowlands, particularly at the Kura River and its principal tributaries. Given the High river flood classification for the Aran zone, crossing protection should be designed against flood events consistent with projected climate conditions for the 2075 horizon. Directional drilling or other trenchless crossing methods should be considered at major crossings to eliminate exposure to surface scour.

**Seismic vulnerability assessment.** Given the High earthquake classification for the Aran zone (>20% probability of damaging shaking in 50 years), a specific seismic vulnerability assessment of OGB pipeline sections traversing the Aran lowlands should be carried out. This assessment should identify sections at risk from ground liquefaction, permanent ground deformation, and fault proximity, and develop prioritised seismic retrofitting or rerouting measures. Pipeline joints and connections at river crossings are the most seismically sensitive elements.

**Mud volcano geohazard screening.** The pipeline route in the Aran zone should be reviewed against the distribution of known and active mud volcano features. Sections within areas of documented mud volcano activity or ground deformation should be assessed for ground movement risk and considered for route adjustments where technically and economically feasible.

**Operational redundancy.** Given the combination of flood and seismic risk to the OGB pipeline, AZERSU should maintain emergency response protocols and pre-positioned repair capacity for rapid response to pipeline rupture events. Source-switching protocols to maximise supply from alternative sources (Baku I & II, Canal) during OGB outages should be pre-defined.

### Samur–Absheron Canal

The Samur–Absheron Canal requires the most extensive climate adaptation provisions in the project, reflecting its Very High drought risk rating and the multiple compounding hazards assessed in Section [X.6]. The canal is the component most likely to experience operationally significant impacts within the project's operational horizon.

**Canal lining rehabilitation to reduce conveyance losses.** The rehabilitation of deteriorated canal lining sections is the single most important adaptation measure for this component. Reducing seepage losses through improved lining directly increases the volume of water delivered to the Jeyranbatan Reservoir for a given inflow at Tahtakorpu. In a drought scenario where inflows are reduced, every percentage point reduction in conveyance loss translates directly to increased water availability for the Greater Baku system. This measure is included in the current project scope and should be prioritised for sections with the highest measured seepage rates.

**Drought operational protocol for canal management.** A dedicated drought management protocol for the Samur–Absheron Canal should be developed, specifying: (i) trigger thresholds based on Samur River flow levels and Tahtakorpu Reservoir storage, at which demand management and source-switching measures are activated; (ii) coordination arrangements with irrigation water users sharing the Samur allocation, to protect drinking water priority during drought periods; (iii) reservoir drawdown rules for Jeyranbatan to ensure minimum operational storage is maintained; and (iv) communication protocols with AZERSU supply operations and Baku city demand management.

**Evaporation reduction measures.** As temperatures increase, open-water evaporation from the canal surface will become an increasingly significant loss mechanism. Options for reducing evaporation losses — such as floating covers for discrete high-evaporation sections, or conversion of open channel sections to closed conduit — should be assessed as part of the detailed design process, particularly for the Absheron section exposed to high wind and solar radiation. A cost–benefit analysis should compare the capital cost of conversion against the long-term operational benefit in terms of water savings under projected climate scenarios.

**Slope and embankment stabilisation in the Guba-Khachmaz zone.** The upper canal corridor passes through the High landslide zone identified in ThinkHazard! for Guba-Khachmaz. Canal embankments in the piedmont section should be assessed for slope stability under both current and projected future conditions (increased intense precipitation events). Vulnerable sections should be stabilised through embankment reinforcement, drainage improvement, and revegetation with deep-rooted species.

**Sediment and debris management.** The projected increase in extreme precipitation intensity may increase sediment and debris loads in the canal catchment. Sedimentation basins and debris screening upstream of the canal intake at Tahtakorpu should be assessed for capacity against projected future sediment loads, and a maintenance programme established to maintain intake and conveyance efficiency.
