# Existing Climate Conditions

## Regional Context

The Greater Baku area and the Absheron Peninsula are situated on the western shore of the Caspian Sea in eastern Azerbaijan, at approximately 40°N latitude. The project infrastructure spans three climatically distinct geographic sub-regions: (i) the **Absheron Peninsula**, a low-lying coastal plain with elevations generally below 100 m above sea level, where the Jeyranbatan Water Treatment Plant, Jeyranbatan Reservoir, and the Saray and Sharg reservoirs are located; (ii) the **Aran lowlands** (Kura–Araz depression), a broad alluvial plain at low elevations, traversed by the Oghuz–Gabala–Baku pipeline and the Kura River source intake; and (iii) the **Guba-Khachmaz** zone in northern Azerbaijan, at the southern piedmont of the Greater Caucasus range, where the Baku I and II pipelines originate at the Sollar springs and the Samur–Absheron Canal begins. The climate baseline and trend analysis in this section focuses on the Absheron Peninsula as the primary project location; climate characteristics of the Aran and Guba-Khachmaz zones are discussed where relevant to the specific pipeline components.

The climate of the Absheron Peninsula is classified as **BSk** (cold semi-arid steppe) under the Köppen–Geiger classification system. The defining characteristics of this class — low annual precipitation relative to potential evapotranspiration, hot dry summers, and cold winters — are a product of the region's continental position, the rain-shadow effect of the Greater Caucasus Mountains to the north and west, and the moderating thermal influence of the Caspian Sea. Dry continental air masses from the Central Asian interior dominate for much of the year, suppressing convective precipitation in summer. The Aran lowlands share a similar BSk to BSh classification, while the Guba-Khachmaz zone, at higher elevation and in closer proximity to the Caucasus range, receives substantially more precipitation and is classified as a temperate oceanic to humid continental climate (Cfa/Dfb transition).

The Absheron Peninsula experiences a semi-arid climate characterised by hot, dry summers and mild, relatively wet winters. The mean annual temperature over the reference period is **14.3°C**, with July and August being the warmest months (mean temperatures of 26.0°C and 25.8°C respectively) and January and February the coldest (both 3.7°C mean), yielding a mean annual temperature range of approximately 22°C that reflects the region's continental character moderated by proximity to the Caspian Sea. Average maximum temperatures exceed 30°C in July and August, with implications for water demand, evaporation from the open Samur–Absheron Canal, and operational conditions at the Jeyranbatan Water Treatment Plant. Annual precipitation totals approximately **307 mm**, placing the area firmly within the semi-arid range. Precipitation is strongly seasonal: autumn and early winter are the wettest period, with October (36.9 mm) and November (36.7 mm) receiving the highest monthly totals, while July (8.4 mm) and August (9.6 mm) are the driest months. Approximately 61% of annual precipitation falls in the October–March half-year, with only 39% occurring during the warmer April–September period. This inverse correlation between temperature and precipitation — the driest months coinciding with peak temperatures and evapotranspiration demand — is a key characteristic of the BSk climate and has direct relevance to source water availability for the water supply system.
