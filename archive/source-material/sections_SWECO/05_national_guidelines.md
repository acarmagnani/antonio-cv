# National Climate and Water Sector Guidelines

## Azerbaijan's Climate Commitments

Azerbaijan ratified the Paris Agreement in 2016 and submitted its Updated Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) to the UNFCCC in 2021. The updated NDC commits Azerbaijan to a reduction of greenhouse gas emissions of **35% below 1990 levels by 2030** on an unconditional basis, increasing to **40%** subject to international support. While the NDC focuses primarily on mitigation, it explicitly identifies the water resources sector as one of the key areas of climate vulnerability in Azerbaijan, alongside agriculture, energy, coastal zones, and human health. The adaptation component of the NDC acknowledges that Azerbaijan's semi-arid regions — including the Absheron Peninsula and the Kura-Araz lowlands — face increasing risks from rising temperatures, reduced precipitation, and growing water demand, and calls for strengthened resilience of water supply and irrigation infrastructure.

Azerbaijan is also party to the UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD), recognising the country's exposure to land degradation and water scarcity in its drylands, and participates in regional climate cooperation frameworks under the UNFCCC Central Asia and Caucasus grouping. National reporting to the UNFCCC through Biennial Update Reports has consistently highlighted water supply reliability and agricultural irrigation as the sectors most exposed to near-term climate impacts.

## Water Sector Strategy and Policy Framework

The national water supply sector in Azerbaijan is governed primarily by the Ministry of Ecology and Natural Resources and administered operationally through **Azərsu Joint Stock Company (AZERSU)**, the state utility responsible for urban and peri-urban water supply and wastewater services. AZERSU manages the Greater Baku water supply system, including the assets covered by this project, and is the primary project counterpart for the EBRD-financed investment.

Azerbaijan has adopted a series of State Programmes targeting improvement of water supply and sanitation services. These programmes have prioritised: (i) expansion of safe drinking water access to urban and peri-urban populations; (ii) reduction of non-revenue water (NRW) through infrastructure rehabilitation and network management; (iii) modernisation of water treatment facilities; and (iv) improvement of water use efficiency. Reduction of NRW is a particularly important policy target given the system's estimated high physical water losses, which exacerbate supply deficits during drought periods and reduce the effective yield of existing water sources.

The National Water Strategy and associated sector plans recognise the Samur River and the Samur–Absheron Canal as critical strategic water infrastructure for the Absheron Peninsula, and include provisions for the rehabilitation of canal sections and improvement of conveyance efficiency. The Oghuz–Gabala–Baku pipeline, commissioned in 2010, was developed specifically to diversify Baku's water supply away from sole dependence on surface water sources vulnerable to seasonal and drought-related variability. These policy investments reflect an existing awareness within the national framework of the need to build resilience into the water supply system against climate and demand pressures.

## Alignment with the Project and Adaptation Measures

The Baku Integrated Drinking Water Supply Project is directly aligned with Azerbaijan's national climate and water sector policy objectives. The project's core components — rehabilitation of transmission infrastructure, reconstruction of the Jeyranbatan WTP, and canal rehabilitation — address the NRW reduction and supply reliability targets embedded in the State Programmes. By reducing physical losses, diversifying water sources, and modernising treatment capacity, the project improves the overall resilience of the Greater Baku system against the growing climate pressures identified in Sections [X.2]–[X.4].

The adaptation measures identified in Section [X.8] are consistent with the adaptation priorities set out in Azerbaijan's Updated NDC, specifically the strengthening of water supply infrastructure resilience in semi-arid regions. The project's EBRD financing framework also requires alignment with the Bank's Environmental and Social Policy and its Climate Resilience Framework, ensuring that climate risks are systematically assessed and addressed as part of project design. The present Climate Risk Assessment fulfils this requirement and provides the technical basis for incorporating climate resilience into detailed design and operations planning.
