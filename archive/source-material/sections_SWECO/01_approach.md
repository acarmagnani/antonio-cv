# Climate Risk Assessment

## Approach to Climate Change Assessment

To ensure that the Project meets the required standards for climate resilience, a dedicated Climate Risk Assessment has been carried out following the methodological approach outlined in the JASPERS Guidance, aligned with EBRD requirements. The assessment follows a structured process:

- Establishment of the climate baseline for the project area using observed data (1991–2020)
- Analysis of historical climate trends based on long-term datasets (1951–2020)
- Assessment of future climate projections under different emissions scenarios (SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5)
- Identification of key climate hazards likely to affect the infrastructure
- Assessment of **exposure**: the degree to which each infrastructure component is located in an area that could be physically affected by a hazard, considering geographic location, elevation, hydrology, and land use
- Assessment of **vulnerability**: the susceptibility of each asset to sustain damage or disruption when exposed
- Assignment of overall **climate risk ratings** by combining hazard magnitude, exposure, and vulnerability for each hazard–component pair
- Formulation of **adaptation options** to mitigate risk and enhance resilience

The climate data used in the assessment was sourced from the World Bank Climate Change Knowledge Portal, which provides access to the CRU TS v4.06 database from the University of East Anglia and to CMIP6-based multi-model projections. Historical observed data cover the reference period 1991–2020. Future projections are referenced against the CMIP6 model baseline period 1995–2014. Risk assessments for site-specific hazards additionally incorporate elevation profiles, hydrological flow paths, and ThinkHazard classifications.

Future climate projections are based on the CMIP6 (Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 6) multi-model ensemble. In line with the JASPERS EC Climate Proofing Technical Guidance, three Shared Socioeconomic Pathway (SSP) scenarios have been selected to cover the full range of the project's exposure horizon:

- **SSP1-2.6**: a low-emissions scenario, used to characterise near-term climate conditions at the start of operations (approximately 2030). This scenario assumes strong mitigation efforts and a transition to sustainable development pathways.
- **SSP2-4.5**: an intermediate emissions scenario broadly consistent with the upper bound of current Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) through 2030. It is used to characterise mid-term exposure up to 2050, representing a best-estimate warming of approximately 2.7°C by 2100 relative to 1850–1900 levels.
- **SSP3-7.0**: a high-emissions reference scenario with limited additional climate policy action. It is used to characterise long-term exposure beyond 2050, providing a stress-test of infrastructure performance under more severe future climate outcomes.

The analysis uses **2025**, **2050**, and **2075** as key time horizons, reflecting the expected construction, operational, and end-of-design-life phases of the project components.
