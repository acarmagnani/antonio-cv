# Climate Risk Ratings

## Risk Rating Methodology

Climate risk ratings are assigned for the 33 hazard–component combinations that passed the preliminary screening in Table [X].8, by combining three factors: **hazard magnitude** (severity and projected change of the hazard), **exposure** (the degree to which the component is physically situated in the hazard zone), and **vulnerability** (the susceptibility of the component to sustain damage or operational disruption when exposed to the hazard). The exposure and vulnerability ratings for each screened-in combination are set out in Table [X].9.

Risk ratings are expressed on a four-level scale consistent with JASPERS EC Climate Proofing Technical Guidance. The definitions for each risk level are given in Table [X].10. The logic for combining exposure and vulnerability ratings is set out in the combining matrix in Table [X].11.

An exception applies where the hazard magnitude is assessed as Very High: in this case, the risk level is elevated to Very High irrespective of the exposure–vulnerability matrix outcome. This applies to a single combination in this assessment: drought/reduced water availability for the Samur–Absheron Canal, where the SPEI-based hazard magnitude is Very High (Section [X.4.5]).

## Risk Level Definitions

**Table [X].10:** Risk Level Definitions

| Risk Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **Low** | Minor or no impact on performance; existing measures provide adequate protection. |
| **Medium** | Noticeable operational impact; adaptation measures may be required in the medium term. |
| **High** | Significant operational disruption or damage likely; adaptation measures required in the short term. |
| **Very High** | Critical impact on functionality and/or safety; urgent adaptation measures required. |

## Risk Combining Matrix

**Table [X].11:** Risk Combining Matrix: Exposure × Vulnerability

| | **Vulnerability Low** | **Vulnerability Medium** | **Vulnerability High** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exposure Low** | Low | Low | Medium |
| **Exposure Medium** | Low | Medium | High |
| **Exposure High** | Medium | High | High |

## Summary

The risk rating matrix reveals a differentiated climate risk profile across the five infrastructure components. The **Samur–Absheron Canal** carries the highest overall climate risk, with a Very High rating for drought/reduced water availability — the most severe rating in the assessment — reflecting its status as an open surface-water conveyance system wholly dependent on catchment precipitation and river flows, in a region where the SPEI moisture balance is projected to deteriorate towards moderate-to-severe drought conditions under higher emissions scenarios by mid-to-late century. High ratings for extreme heat (evaporation losses), landslide (upper canal corridor in the Guba-Khachmaz zone), and strong winds/dust (Absheron section) further indicate that this component requires the most comprehensive climate-resilience provisions in the project.

The **Jeyranbatan WTP** carries High risk ratings for both extreme heat and drought. Heat risk arises from direct impacts on treatment process performance, equipment reliability, and worker safety during increasingly frequent high-temperature periods. Drought risk reflects the plant's dependence on the Jeyranbatan Reservoir, which is supplied primarily via the canal and is therefore vulnerable to the same source water reductions. These two risks are interlinked: drought reduces reservoir storage while heat simultaneously increases treatment demand and deteriorates source water quality.

The **OGB Pipeline** carries High risk ratings for river flooding, earthquake, and extreme precipitation, reflecting the severe combined hazard environment of the Aran lowlands — the highest seismic zone in the project area (ThinkHazard! High), situated on an active floodplain prone to Kura River flooding. These hazards are particularly significant because disruption to the OGB pipeline would cut off a major source of high-quality groundwater for Baku.

The **Baku I and II Pipelines** carry a High landslide rating, reflecting the High ThinkHazard! landslide classification for the Guba-Khachmaz zone where the pipelines originate. Slope instability in the Greater Caucasus piedmont poses a risk of pipe rupture or displacement, particularly in deteriorated legacy sections. All other screened-in hazards for this component are rated Medium.

The **Saray–Sharg Pipeline** carries a Medium risk rating across all five screened-in hazards, consistent with its status as a new-construction project on the relatively low-hazard Absheron Peninsula. Climate resilience can be incorporated from the outset through alignment selection, design standards, and construction quality, keeping intrinsic vulnerability low across all hazard categories.
