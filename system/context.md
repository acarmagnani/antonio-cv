## Level and fit calibration

Rules that keep go/no-go verdicts from going wrong. A job title on its own does not settle the question of level:

- **Where he actually sits.** 
  M.Arch completed July 2026, age 27, roughly four years of professional experience. Nearly all of it ran concurrently with full-time study, including two contractually full-time consulting roles taken during the master's. Pitch as "around four years of experience, including two full-time consulting roles held during the master's". Role-by-role detail lives in `content_base.yaml`, do not restate it here.

- **Graduate programmes are IN.** 
  Graduate schemes filter on time since graduation, not on volume of experience, and he graduated in July 2026, so he is inside the eligibility window for the 2026/2027 intakes. Prior internships and concurrent work make him strong within that pool, not overqualified for it. At Big Four, Arup, Ramboll and similar firms these structured tracks are often the only realistic door for someone without prior tenure there.

- **Target band:** 
  Post-master entry through roughly three years. Apply to both bands in parallel, direct-entry Consultant where he clears it and graduate schemes as the reliable floor, and let the market show where his ceiling is. Postings asking for 5+ years are a hard skip.

- **Big Four titles.** 
  At EY, Deloitte, KPMG and PwC, "Consultant" is the post-master level, not a junior one (junior is "Analyst" or "Associate"). Never skip a Big Four "Consultant" posting on level grounds. These firms fill ESG mostly through graduate intake, and the experienced-hire route usually expects an audit or accounting background, or prior tenure at another Big Four.

- **Python boundary.** 
  His Python is analytical and scripting (QGIS, machine learning, Grasshopper, Excel automation), not production software engineering. Proptech and contech postings for "Software Engineer" or "Full-stack" are a stretch however well the domain fits. Data, product, implementation, and solutions roles at the same companies are not.

- **Real estate splits in two.** 
  Advisory and consultancy (CBRE, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield, valuation, research, project monitoring) is a realistic fit. The investment side (real estate private equity, funds, acquisitions analyst) normally expects a finance or banking background and is a harder sell, but Antonio is genuinely interested and wants these roles surfaced anyway. Name the gap honestly in the verdict, do not filter them out.

- **Client and owner side counts.** 
  Municipalities, public infrastructure agencies, utilities, and large corporates with real estate portfolios (his Itaú-Unibanco role was exactly this) do the upstream strategic work he wants and do hire architects. A valid direction, not a detour.
