Dear Hiring Team at Capgemini Invent,

I trained as an architect in Brazil, and what kept pulling at me was never the building but the question standing in front of it. Why here, on what evidence, and had anyone actually checked. That took me upstream, out of drawing and into analysis, and eventually to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme about using data and technology to improve decisions in an industry that still makes most of them on instinct. A practice that describes its work as validated by science and data is starting from the same premise.

Most of what I have done since has been consulting. At Sweco I supported teams delivering water, energy and transport projects financed by the World Bank, EBRD, KfW and EIB. I wrote the technical and methodology sections of competitive international tenders, setting out how the work would actually be delivered. I built a pricing tool that parametrised expert fee bands and project economics so bid decisions rested on numbers. And on a field mission across Baku's water system I sat with national agencies and government counterparts to agree rehabilitation priorities and gather technical data nobody had assembled.

The data side ran alongside it. At Ramboll Management Consulting I built an automated risk assessment in Excel that pulled supply chain data into a single dashboard and reprioritised as inputs changed, benchmarked a client against its competitors to find where it was exposed and where it could push, and integrated AI research tools into due diligence to cover volumes we could not have reached by hand. Separately, a property valuation model I built finished in the top 2% of its competition. Earlier still I co-founded and ran a studio serving more than ten clients, doing my own scoping, pricing and hiring.

My master's is not in business or computer science. It is a data and technology degree carrying an architecture name, and a practice built out of strategic, creative and scientific work is where I want to put it to use.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
