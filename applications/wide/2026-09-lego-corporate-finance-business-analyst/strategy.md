# Strategy, LEGO Corporate Finance Business Analyst

**Positioning:** the person who sits between the people who need a number and the people who build the tool that produces it, and who has repeatedly built that tool himself.

**Profile chosen:** `profile.proptech-real-estate`. It leads with feasibility and financial analysis, which is what a Corporate Reporting & Planning reader is scanning for, and still names the data and modelling skills. The honest tension is that it is anchored in real estate while the team is corporate finance; the letter carries the bridge, not the CV.

**Threads to lead with:**

1. **He builds the tools finance people use, then gets them adopted.** The Grasshopper plugin at Isay Weinfeld is the cleanest evidence: he wrote it, presented it in a studio workshop, and supported colleagues through adoption across 10+ projects. That is requirements, build, training and change adoption in one bullet pair, which is the shape of this job. The VBA inventory dashboard at Itaú (replacing a manual process), the automated Excel risk system at Ramboll, and the private-client financial analysis tool that ingests statements and categorises transactions all repeat the pattern: manual process in, decision dashboard out.

2. **He has done the product-side half of it too.** Two years advising a built-environment software startup on product direction, UI and UX, testing releases and handing back improved scripts. That is the business-analyst-to-developer relationship the posting describes, from the other chair.

**The gap, stated plainly:** no OneStream or enterprise performance management, and no formal requirements/UAT title. The letter should not pretend otherwise. What it can say is that every tool he has built started as someone else's messy process and ended as something colleagues actually used.

**Bucket:** `wide/`. The level is right and the work is genuinely finance-adjacent, but the seat is enterprise finance systems, not the built environment, so it does not count toward the target analysis.
