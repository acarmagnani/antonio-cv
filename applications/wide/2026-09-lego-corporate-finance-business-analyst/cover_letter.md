Dear Hiring Team at the LEGO Group,

I trained as an architect, but the part of the work that held me was always the step before the drawings, where somebody has to turn a messy situation into numbers other people can decide on. That pulled me upstream, first into feasibility and financial analysis, then into building the tools that produce those numbers, and eventually to Copenhagen for an M.Arch in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme about using data and technology to improve how decisions get made in a slow-moving industry. A Business Analyst seat in Corporate Reporting & Planning, sitting between finance colleagues and the people building the planning tools, is that same work in a different industry.

At Isay Weinfeld Architects I wrote a Grasshopper plugin in Python that generates bespoke staircases from a floor plan, taking a task that ran to days down to roughly twenty minutes. Writing it was the easy half. The rest was sitting with the architects who had been doing it by hand, working out what they actually needed rather than what they first asked for, then presenting the tool in a studio workshop and staying close to colleagues while it went into more than ten projects. Adoption, not the code, was what decided whether any of it counted.

The pattern has repeated since. At Itaú-Unibanco I replaced a manual furniture inventory with a VBA dashboard that tracked stock across locations and sped up procurement for new fit-outs. At Ramboll I built a fully automated risk assessment in Excel that consolidated supply chain data into a single dashboard and reprioritised salient risks as the inputs changed. And for two years I advised a built-environment software startup on product direction, UI and UX, testing releases and handing back improved Revit and Dynamo scripts, which is this role seen from the developer's side of the table.

I have not worked in OneStream, and the financial planning landscape is something I would be learning from the outside. What I bring instead is the habit of turning someone else's manual process into something colleagues trust enough to use, and Corporate Reporting & Planning is expanding exactly that capability over the next year and a half.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
