# Strategy, Boston Consulting Group, Consultant

**Positioning:** a consultant already trained twice in Copenhagen, at Ramboll Management Consulting and at Sweco, who structures an open problem, makes it quantitative, and lands it with people senior enough to act on it, and who brings a domain (built environment, infrastructure, sustainability) that most generalist consultants have to learn on the job.

**Profile chosen:** `profile.esg-consulting`. It is the only variant that opens on consulting rather than on real estate or parametric design, and it names the due-diligence frameworks and structured analytical tools in Excel and Python that carry the analytical claim.

**Bucket:** `wide`. Strategy consulting is a different field, tried opportunistically, and BCG is the hardest screen he will face. Not counted in the gap analysis.

**Seniority read, and it is not the usual one.** On BCG's ladder, Consultant is the advanced-degree entry rung (Associate is the undergraduate entry, Project Leader comes after), so the rung itself is correct for someone with a Master's. The problem is the experience bar underneath it. The posting asks for 3-5+ years in top tier consulting, industry or strategic roles, and of his roughly three full-time years only about ten months are consulting, one of those an internship. The rest is architecture practice. Add the pool he is competing against, MBAs from target schools and people leaving McKinsey and Bain, plus a case interview and a numerical test at the door. `notes/context.md` already rates EY-Parthenon as unlikely for the same structural reason, and BCG screens harder than that.

**Realistic odds: low.** Worth sending, not worth calibrating expectations on.

**Flag before he sends it:** the posting does not name an office. Any EU office is clean on eligibility. London would need sponsorship, which BCG does provide, but it changes the process.

**CV note:** written by hand, no preset, and deliberately close to the Novo Nordisk Consulting selection. Two small differences. `ramboll.frameworks-anchor.v2` replaces v1 because it leads on identifying and prioritising material risks rather than on human rights specifically, and `ramboll.colt-salience-tool.v2` replaces the client workshop bullet because BCG names financial modelling and the automated Excel system is the better evidence, while the coaching ask is already answered by the plugin adoption bullet.

**Threads to lead with:**

- **Ramboll Management Consulting is a management consultancy, stated plainly.** Benchmarking a client against its competitors and turning the gaps into positioning advice. An automated Excel risk system consolidating scattered business-unit data into one dashboard. An impact assessment tool mapping 40+ risks with severity scoring. An internal market study on 20+ critical raw material supply chains connecting regulation to business development targets.
- **The domain is the differentiator, not the application.** Apply as a consultant, let the built environment, infrastructure and sustainability be the reason he is worth interviewing over another generalist.
- **Learning an unfamiliar system fast, which is the actual job.** The Baku field mission, assessing asset condition through inspections and interviews and aligning priorities with national water agencies and government counterparts, in a country he had never worked in.
- **Coaching and adoption, evidenced.** The Python plugin cut a task from days to around twenty minutes, and he then presented it internally and drove adoption across 10+ projects. Plus hiring and running a studio with 10+ clients.
- **Name the gap once.** He is not coming from a strategy house. The letter says so in one sentence and then moves to what he has instead. Do not apologise twice, and do not raise doubts about the case interview, which is the exact screen they are looking for a reason to fail him on.
- **Do not claim:** strategy-house training, MBA, M&A or due diligence in a transaction sense, or any implementation of a large transformation programme.
