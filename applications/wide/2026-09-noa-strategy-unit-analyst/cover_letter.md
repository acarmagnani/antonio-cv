Dear Hiring Team at NoA,

I trained as an architect, and the interest that has run alongside that from the beginning is data - using it to understand what has been built and what could be built better. That is what took me from architecture practice into feasibility and analysis, and then to a Master's Degree in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme built around using data and technology to improve the decisions made in real estate and construction. The through line has been consistent, which is that I would rather build the analytical tool that answers a question than argue the question without one. NoA's strategy unit asks that commercially, working out how marketing creates value for a business and testing LLM-based tools on the question, and a unit established this recently is one where the way of working is still open.

My Master's Degree in Architecture Computation gave the analytical side of my work its proper depth. I built a predictive property valuation model using ensemble learning across XGBoost, LightGBM, Ridge and CatBoost that finished in the top two per cent of its competition, and a machine learning tool that generates site-specific development recommendations for buildings in Copenhagen by combining financial, social, sustainability and geospatial data, with an interface that lets non-technical stakeholders adjust the impact drivers and weightings themselves. The second of those mattered more to me than the first, because it forced the modelling to survive contact with people who would never open the code. That is the shape of this role, an analysis owned from brief to finished output and made to land with people who decide commercially rather than technically.

During my time in Ramboll Management Consulting's Responsible Business Conduct team I ran human rights due diligence for corporate clients under the UN Guiding Principles and the OECD Guidelines, and in practice a large part of that was data work. I built a fully automated risk assessment system in Excel that consolidated supply chain risk data into a single dashboard which updated salient risks dynamically, an impact assessment tool that scored more than forty risks by severity so the most material ones surfaced first, and a mapping of the supply chains of over twenty critical raw materials under the EU Critical Raw Materials Act. The common task underneath all of it was taking broad and uneven bodies of evidence and structuring them rigorously enough to produce scored, defensible outputs. Client and third-party data from large brands arrives in the same state, and the structuring done before the modelling is what decides whether the recommendation on the other side holds.

NoA puts strategy, creative and technology work in the same group, and the strategy unit is the part of it accountable for showing what the marketing is actually worth. That is where I want to keep doing the work I find most worthwhile, which is turning large and awkward bodies of data into something a business can decide on.

Best,
Antonio Carmagnani
