# Strategy — Business Intelligence Associate, Maersk Tankers

**Positioning:** An analyst who has spent his whole career building the reporting layer other people make decisions on, in Excel, VBA, Python and QGIS, and who wants to do that full time on a proper BI stack.

**Bucket:** `wide/`. The seat is at his level and Copenhagen is home, but the industry is shipping and there is no route from a Data Insights team at a tanker company back toward real estate. It is a data application, not a step toward the target.

**Profile chosen:** `profile.proptech`. The alternative, `profile.real-estate-esg-data`, is the only one that names Power BI in the text, which is tempting for a keyword screen. It loses on everything else: it opens on real estate development and sustainability consulting and closes on real estate, whereas this profile opens on data analysis and closes on technology and data. Power BI still appears in the skills block, so the keyword is on the page either way.

**Threads to lead with:**

1. **The tools he built because nobody else would.** The automated risk-assessment dashboard at Ramboll that consolidated supply chain data and re-ranked salient risks dynamically, the VBA inventory dashboard at Itaú-Unibanco that replaced a manual process, and the financial analysis tool that ingests statements and PDF exports, categorises transactions and renders them into a decision dashboard. That last one is the closest thing on the CV to an ETL pipeline feeding a report, and it is the strongest single piece of evidence for this job.
2. **Stakeholders on both sides.** Most of his analytical work existed to be read by someone who was not technical: lenders reading climate risk ratings, a bid team reading a pricing model, procurement reading an inventory dashboard, a corporate client reading a salience score. The posting asks twice for exactly that, and it is where an architect-trained analyst is genuinely stronger than a pure data graduate.

**The honest gap:** he has never held a BI title, and the two named requirements he cannot evidence from the record are Power BI dashboards shipped in a professional setting and production ETL pipelines. SQL and Power BI are on his skills list and he can work in both, but the CV shows Excel, VBA and Python as the places he actually built things. Do not claim BI experience in the letter; claim the underlying work and the fluency, and let the tools sit in the skills block.
