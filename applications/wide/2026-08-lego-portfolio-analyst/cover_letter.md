Dear Hiring Team at the LEGO Group,

I trained as an architect in Brazil, but what kept pulling me was the step before the drawing, where someone has to decide what gets built and why. That took me from São Paulo to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme built on data, modelling and code. Looking back at every job since, the through-line is not the buildings. It is that in each one I ended up building the thing that made a messy set of moving parts visible enough to choose from.

At Ramboll Management Consulting I did that twice. I built a fully automated risk-assessment system in Excel that pulled scattered supply chain data from across a client's business units into a single dashboard, updating dynamically as inputs changed, so the salient risks surfaced on their own instead of being reassembled by hand each time. Separately I built an impact assessment tool that mapped more than forty risks across a client's supply chain and scored each one by severity, which is how a long undifferentiated list turns into a ranked one people can act on.

The same pattern shows up earlier. At Itaú-Unibanco I built a VBA dashboard tracking the bank's corporate inventory across locations, replacing a manual process and speeding up procurement for new buildings. At Sweco I built a pricing model that parametrizes expert fee bands and project economics so the team can judge which bids are worth chasing. And at an architecture studio in São Paulo I wrote a Python tool that cut a modelling task from several days to around twenty minutes, then presented it internally and supported its adoption across more than ten projects.

A portfolio spanning sustainability projects, new technologies and play experiences is exactly the kind of mixed set where those comparisons are hard and worth getting right, and that is the part of the work I want to be doing.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
