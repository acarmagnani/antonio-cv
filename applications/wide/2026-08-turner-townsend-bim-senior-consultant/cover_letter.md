Dear Hiring Team at Turner & Townsend,

I trained as an architect and spent most of my practice years inside the model rather than beside it, coordinating disciplines and building the tools that made the modelling faster. That interest is what took me from São Paulo to Copenhagen for an M.Arch in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme about using computation to change how buildings get delivered rather than how they look. A digital team implementing BIM on a data centre, on behalf of the client, is where that background does the most work.

At Isay Weinfeld I coordinated multidisciplinary inputs, structural, HVAC, electrical and lighting, into a single Revit model, and produced the executive and technical documentation and coordination models across project phases. I have been on the receiving end of model assurance, which is a useful place to have stood before doing it to somebody else. I also wrote a Grasshopper plugin in Python that generates bespoke staircases from a floor plan, cutting the modelling from days to about twenty minutes, then presented it in a studio workshop and supported colleagues adopting it across more than ten projects. Getting a project team to trust a new way of working is most of the job, and it is slower and more human than the technology suggests.

The assurance habit comes from elsewhere. At Sweco I reviewed engineering designs under FIDIC Yellow Book arrangements, checking specifications against subconsultants' technical drawings, and contributed the technical and methodology sections of international tender proposals. Earlier, at Itaú-Unibanco, I reviewed documentation from architecture and engineering firms against building, accessibility and fire-safety standards before construction could be authorised. I have not worked to ISO 19650, and I would come in reading it properly rather than claiming it.

Turner & Townsend Digital works at the point where information management decides whether a complex asset gets delivered well, and a data centre is about as unforgiving a test of that as exists. I would like to learn this side of it from a team that does it at scale.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
