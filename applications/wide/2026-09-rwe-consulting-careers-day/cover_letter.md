Dear Hiring Team at RWE Consulting,

I trained as an architect, and what progressively drew me beyond the drawings were the questions a design studio rarely stops on - why something is being built, whether it should be built there at all, and what its impact will be over its life. Following those questions took me to a Master's Degree in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy and, within it, specifically toward data, which is the part that makes it possible to answer them with evidence instead of intuition, and from there into sustainability and responsible business consulting. RWE Consulting sits inside that question rather than beside it, since an inhouse team working alongside Corporate Strategy and Sustainability carries the strategic initiatives of a company that has committed to carbon neutrality by 2040 and has to build the assets that get it there.

During my time in Ramboll Management Consulting's Responsible Business Conduct team I ran human rights due diligence for corporate clients under the UN Guiding Principles and the OECD Guidelines, and in practice a large part of that was data work. I built a fully automated risk assessment system in Excel that consolidated supply chain risk data into a single dashboard which updated salient risks dynamically, an impact assessment tool that scored more than forty risks by severity so the most material ones surfaced first, and a mapping of the supply chains of over twenty critical raw materials under the EU Critical Raw Materials Act. The common task underneath all of it was taking broad and uneven bodies of evidence and structuring them rigorously enough to produce scored, defensible outputs. Consulting on the energy transition rests on the same discipline, because a strategic recommendation is worth only as much as the evidence assembled underneath it and the order imposed on that evidence.

At Sweco I supported advisory teams delivering water, energy and transport projects financed by development banks including the World Bank, EBRD, KfW and EIB, and authored climate risk assessments for major water supply projects under JASPERS and EBRD climate-proofing guidance, screening hazards against projection data and rating exposure and vulnerability asset by asset. Working to lender standards taught me that an assessment is only useful if it survives review by someone with money at stake, which changes how you evidence every claim you make. Renewable generation is held to that same test, since projects of that size are financed, permitted and built on assessments that outside parties are entitled to check line by line.

RWE produces electricity from renewable sources at a scale few companies reach, and its inhouse consultancy works on the initiatives that decide how the transition is actually delivered rather than how it is described. That is where I want to keep building sustainability work that is measurable, backed by evidence, and able to withstand scrutiny.

Best,
Antonio Carmagnani
