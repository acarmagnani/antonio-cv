Dear Hiring Team at Statkraft,

I trained as an architect, and the part of the work that held me was the step before the drawings, deciding what should be built, where, and whether the numbers behind it survive contact with reality. Chasing that upstream question took me from practice in Brazil to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation, and then into infrastructure and sustainability consulting, where it gets asked of energy systems rather than buildings. A team whose job is to form a house view on how the costs and volumes of power technologies develop is the sharpest version of that question I have found.

At Sweco I support consulting teams delivering water, energy and transport projects financed by the World Bank, EBRD, KfW and EIB. I built a pricing tool that parametrized expert fee bands and project economics so that bid configurations could be tested for financial viability before anyone committed to them, wrote climate risk assessments screening hazards against projections data and rating exposure and vulnerability asset by asset, and reviewed engineering designs against subconsultants' specifications under FIDIC arrangements. Taking a technology, a market and a set of uncertain inputs and turning them into a number someone can act on is the work I keep coming back to.

The quantitative thread runs through the rest. At Ramboll I mapped the supply chains of 20+ critical raw materials under the EU Critical Raw Materials Act, profiling producing companies, sectors and geographies from industry databases, which is the input layer sitting underneath any cost curve for wind, solar or storage. I also built an automated risk system in Excel that pulled scattered supply chain data into one dashboard that updated itself. In Python I wrote a Grasshopper plugin that cut a modelling task from days to around twenty minutes, and I placed in the top 2% of a house price prediction competition using ensemble methods (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost).

I have not worked on wind power or power market forecasting directly, and I would be learning that domain from a team that knows it well. What I bring is the method underneath it and a real interest in the transition. Statkraft has been at this for a century, and building a career on the long view of the energy system is the direction I want.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
