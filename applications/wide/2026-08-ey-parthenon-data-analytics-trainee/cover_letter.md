Dear Hiring Team at EY-Parthenon,

I trained as an architect in Brazil, but the part of the work that pulled me hardest was always the step before the drawings, the question of what a site is actually worth and what is driving that number. Alongside it I kept a second habit, building the tools to answer it, which is why I moved to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme built around using data to make better decisions about real estate. EYP Data & Analytics runs that same instinct on transactions, and on datasets far larger than any single asset produces.

At OSPA I produced more than 15 feasibility studies for developers in São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Porto Alegre and Miami, sizing investment and return against buildable area, unit mix, price per square metre and parking, and reading municipal master plans and land-use law to find where the regulatory position could be pushed in the client's favour. Every one of those studies began as a pile of unstructured material, zoning text, market comparables, site constraints, and had to end as a number someone could commit capital against.

The tooling side has run in parallel. I built a predictive property valuation model using XGBoost, LightGBM, Ridge and CatBoost that finished in the top 2% of its competition, and a machine learning tool for Copenhagen that merges financial, social, sustainability and geospatial data into site-specific development recommendations, with an interface that lets non-technical stakeholders adjust the weightings themselves. At Itaú-Unibanco, a VBA dashboard I wrote replaced a manual inventory process across the bank's locations. Getting a model into the hands of people who will not open the code is usually the harder half of the job.

A Nordic team drawing on data science, engineering, finance and accounting is where an architect who codes stops being an odd profile and starts being useful. I want to spend the next years on transaction analytics, and Helsinki is where I would like to start.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
