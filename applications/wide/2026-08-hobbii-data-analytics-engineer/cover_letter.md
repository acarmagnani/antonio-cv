Dear Hiring Team at Hobbii,

I trained as an architect, but in most places I have worked I ended up being the person who built the tool. Never because it was in the job description. Someone needed an answer, the data behind it sat in a spreadsheet nobody quite trusted, and I went and fixed it. That pull toward the question before the drawing is why I moved from Brazil to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation, and it is why a small data team that owns the definitions a whole company measures itself by is the kind of place I want to work.

At Isay Weinfeld, one of Brazil's most prominent architecture studios, I wrote a Grasshopper plugin in Python that generated bespoke staircases straight from a floor plan, cutting a task that took days down to around twenty minutes. Building it was the easy half. The rest was running a studio workshop, sitting with colleagues who had never scripted anything, and making the logic legible enough that they trusted what came out. It ended up in more than ten projects, which had more to do with the workshop than with the code.

The pattern repeated in consulting. At Ramboll I built a fully automated risk assessment system in Excel that consolidated supply chain data into a single dashboard updating dynamically, alongside a human rights impact assessment tool that mapped 40+ risks with severity scoring to decide which ones actually mattered. Agreeing on that scoring, and defending it to the people who would act on it, was most of the work. Earlier, at Itaú-Unibanco, I replaced a manual inventory process with a VBA dashboard that the procurement team ran on.

I have not worked in Cube, dbt, or Redshift. I have spent years on the problem underneath them, which is getting an organisation to agree on what a number means and then trust it enough to decide with it. Hobbii is scaling across twelve markets on numbers a lot of people need to rely on at once. Building that kind of shared, defensible ground is the work I want to be doing.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
