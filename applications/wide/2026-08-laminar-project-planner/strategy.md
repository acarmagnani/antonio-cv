# Strategy — Laminar Projects, Project Planner

**Positioning:** an architect who moved to the delivery side of complex international infrastructure and who, everywhere he has worked, has built the tool instead of repeating the workaround. Laminar is a consultancy that also builds product, so the tool-building track record is the strongest card, and it maps directly onto their "make scalable solutions" and "Impact" must-haves.

**Profile chosen:** `profile.infrastructure-consulting`. It leads with real professional work on construction and infrastructure projects, tender through delivery, which is what carries credibility against the "2+ years on construction projects" requirement. The alternative (`profile.proptech`) reads as computational designer and would widen the exact gap this application has.

**Threads to lead with:**

1. **The staircase plugin at Isay Weinfeld.** Python in Grasshopper, staircase modelling from days to around 20 minutes, presented in a studio workshop, adopted by colleagues across 10+ projects. This is the anchor: a scalable, repeatable solution with measurable impact, plus the influence to get people in construction to change how they work. It answers three of Laminar's four must-haves at once.
2. **Sweco, the delivery side of complex projects.** Engineering design review under FIDIC Yellow Book, a field mission across Baku's water supply system inspecting assets and interviewing operators, stakeholder alignment with national agencies, and a pricing tool parametrizing fee bands for bid decisions. This is where the construction-project exposure and the commercial instinct live.
3. **Itaú-Unibanco, process improvement in Excel.** VBA-powered dashboard replacing a manual inventory process across the bank's locations, plus reviewing technical documentation before construction authorization. Backs the Excel requirement with something built, not just used.

**Gaps not to paper over:** no planning software (Primavera P6, Asta, MS Project) and no track record owning a project schedule. The letter does not claim either. It leans on the "strong interest in data" and "eager to learn complex workflows" side of the posting instead. UK work authorization is a separate open question, flagged to Antonio, not raised in the letter.
