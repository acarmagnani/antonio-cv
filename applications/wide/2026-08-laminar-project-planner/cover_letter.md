Dear Hiring Team at Laminar Projects,

I trained as an architect, but the part of the work that kept pulling me in was never the drawing. It was why a project runs the way it does, and how much of the chaos is just nobody having built the right tool yet. That took me from architecture practice in Brazil to international infrastructure delivery in Copenhagen, and through an M.Arch in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme built on using data and technology to improve construction. A consultancy that delivers complex projects and then turns what it learns into products anyone can use is rare, and it is the version of this industry I want to work in.

At Isay Weinfeld Architects I got tired of watching bespoke staircases eat days of modelling time, so I wrote a Grasshopper plugin in Python that generates them from a floor plan. It cut the work from days to around twenty minutes and opened up geometry we would not have attempted otherwise. The harder half was adoption. I presented it in a studio workshop and worked with colleagues until it was running on more than ten projects, because a tool nobody uses is not a solution. That is the pattern I keep repeating. Find the recurring problem, build something repeatable, then do the unglamorous work of getting people to use it.

At Sweco I have worked on the delivery side of internationally financed water, energy and transport projects, reviewing engineering designs under FIDIC Yellow Book arrangements and joining a field mission across Baku's water supply system to inspect asset condition and interview the agencies running it. I also built a pricing tool that parametrized fee bands and project economics so bid decisions could be tested rather than guessed.

I have not owned a project schedule yet, and I would be learning your planning software from the ground up. What I bring is a construction background, a real appetite for complex workflows and regulation, and a habit of leaving behind something that outlasts the project I built it on.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
