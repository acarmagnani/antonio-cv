Dear Hiring Team at Maersk,

I trained and worked as an architect in Brazil, but the part of real estate that held me was never the drawings. It was the decision before them, why a site is worth building on, what a building costs and returns, and whether the numbers support the plan. Following that took me from design into feasibility and financial analysis, and then to an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, where I learned to answer those questions with data rather than intuition. A Real Estate Category Manager role that treats the portfolio as a strategic input to decisions, backed by data, is the work I have been moving toward.

At OSPA I produced feasibility studies for 15+ mixed-use and residential developments across Brazil and the US. For each I ran early-stage financial feasibility, sizing investment and return against buildable area, unit mix, price per square metre, and parking, and I analyzed municipal master plans and land-use law to find the regulatory moves that maximized a site's value. Then I presented the outcome to the people making the call. Reading an asset's constraints, turning them into numbers, and landing on a recommendation someone can act on is the core of that work, and of this role.

The corporate-portfolio side is familiar too. At Itaú-Unibanco I assessed the environmental and energy performance of buildings the bank was acquiring or leasing, reviewing consumption data, efficiency ratings, and certifications as internal due diligence, and I built a dashboard that tracked the bank's assets across locations to speed up new-building fit-outs. That analytical thread runs through everything I do, from an ensemble model for property valuation that placed in the top 2% of its competition to a tool that turns financial, social, and spatial data into site-specific recommendations.

Maersk sets real estate strategy for more than 400 offices across every region. Doing that with rigor, as a data-driven read of a global portfolio, is where I want to grow.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
