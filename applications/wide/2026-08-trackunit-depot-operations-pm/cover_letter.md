Dear Hiring Team at Trackunit,

I trained and worked as an architect in Brazil, but the parts of the job that held me were rarely the drawings. They were the operational problems around them, how equipment and information actually move on a project, and whether a tool could make that flow better. Following that pull led me to an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, and toward building software for the built environment rather than only drawing it. The Depot Operations role, shaping a product from the ground up for the people getting equipment out to jobsites and back, sits exactly where my domain and that instinct meet.

At Isay Weinfeld, one of Brazil's most prominent studios, I watched colleagues lose days to a repetitive modeling task. I built a Grasshopper plugin in Python that generates bespoke staircases from a floor plan, cutting that work from days to around 20 minutes. The harder part was adoption. I ran a studio workshop and supported colleagues until the tool was used across 10+ projects. That loop, getting close to how people actually work, building the thing that removes the friction, then earning its use, is the one this role runs on.

The operational-data thread runs through the rest of my work. At Itaú-Unibanco I built a VBA dashboard that tracked corporate equipment across locations, replacing a manual process and speeding up procurement, essentially assets moving in and out and staying accounted for. At Sweco I build geospatial databases and a pricing tool that turns project economics into a bid decision, and in my M.Arch. I built a machine-learning tool with an interface that let stakeholders adjust the drivers and read the outputs. Underneath all of it is one skill, turning messy operational data into a decision someone on the ground can act on.

Trackunit is building that for construction at scale, close to a real problem rather than an abstract one. That is where I want to grow.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
