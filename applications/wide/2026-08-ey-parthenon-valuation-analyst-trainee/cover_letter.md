Dear Hiring Team at EY-Parthenon,

I trained as an architect in Brazil, and the part of the work that held me was never the drawings. It was the step before them, working out what a site could carry, what it would cost, and whether the numbers justified building at all. That question pulled me upstream, from design into feasibility and valuation, and eventually from São Paulo to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme built around using data to make better decisions about real estate. Your Valuation and Modelling team values businesses and individual assets and builds the feasibility and financing models behind those decisions. Same question, wider set of things.

As a trainee in real estate product development at OSPA, I produced more than fifteen feasibility studies for developments across Brazil and the United States, sizing investment and return against buildable area, unit mix, and price per square metre to decide whether a site was worth pursuing. At Felipe Hess Architects I ran the financial feasibility alongside the regulatory analysis, testing saleable area and development cost against what each site could legally hold. At Sweco I built a pricing tool for competitive international tenders, parametrizing expert fee bands and project economics so bid decisions rested on something other than instinct. Different assets each time, and the same habit throughout, taking a messy set of constraints, putting numbers on them, and landing on a recommendation someone can act on.

The modelling side is where I have gone furthest. I built a house price prediction model using ensemble methods on property and financial data, finishing in the top two percent of its competition, and I have written Excel and VBA tools other people use, including an automated risk assessment system at Ramboll and a financial analysis tool that turns raw bank statements into a decision dashboard.

I am not a finance graduate, and I am applying knowing that. What I bring instead is several years spent valuing real assets, a genuine ease with models and code, and work across three countries in four languages. EY-Parthenon is where I would learn to do this properly, on businesses rather than buildings.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
