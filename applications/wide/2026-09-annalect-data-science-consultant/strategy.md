# Positioning: Annalect, Consultant, Data Science Consultancy

**One line:** someone who already builds statistical and predictive models on messy real-world data and hands the results to paying clients, arriving from the built environment rather than from economics.

**Profile chosen:** `profile.proptech`. It leads with Python, QGIS and machine learning and ends on building data tools that turn data into decisions, which is the closest thing in the base to Annalect's own description of the job. The real estate framing stays, because it is true and because it is what makes him a different candidate from the cand.polit pile, not something to hide.

**Threads to lead with**

1. **Models built end to end, not coursework.** The house price prediction model (ensemble learning, XGBoost and LightGBM, top 2% of the competition) is the single most legible proof for this team: structured data, feature engineering, a statistical model that had to actually perform. The master's thesis and the Copenhagen recommendation tool extend it into integrating multiple datasets and shipping something a non-technical user can steer.
2. **The full project lifecycle, with the client at the end of it.** Ramboll's automated risk assessment consolidating supply chain data into a live dashboard, Sweco's pricing tool parametrising fee bands and project economics, and the Itaú VBA inventory dashboard all follow the shape Annalect describes: collect the data, structure it, model it, deliver something someone decides with. OSPA adds presenting the outcome to the client directly.

**The honest gap:** no R (Python, SQL, VBA and Power BI instead, and the posting explicitly accepts that with willingness to learn), no formal econometrics, and no marketing or media domain knowledge. The application does not pretend otherwise; the argument is that method and client delivery transfer, and that the media landscape is learnable.
