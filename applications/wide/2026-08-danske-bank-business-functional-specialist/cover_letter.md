Dear Hiring Team at Danske Bank,

I trained as an architect, and the part of the work I kept drifting toward was never the drawing. It was sitting between the people who knew what was needed and the people who had to build it, and making sure the thing that got built was the thing that was actually meant. That pull is what took me from São Paulo to Copenhagen for an M.Arch in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme about using technology to modernise an industry that runs on old processes. Modernising the customer and account domains of a bank is a bigger version of the same problem.

For two years I advised a software startup in the built environment on product direction, UI and UX, and applied AI. I tested releases, and instead of returning only opinions I handed the team back improved Revit and Dynamo scripts, so what they got was a working version of what I meant. Working out what a user genuinely needs, saying it in terms a development team can act on, and staying with it through the release is the part of that work I would want to keep doing.

I have also been on the building side of it. At Isay Weinfeld I wrote a Grasshopper plugin in Python that generates bespoke staircases from a floor plan, cutting the modelling from days to about twenty minutes, then presented it in a studio workshop and supported colleagues adopting it across more than ten projects. At Ramboll I built an automated risk-assessment system in Excel that pulled scattered supply chain data into a single dashboard that updated itself. Earlier, at Itaú-Unibanco, I replaced a manual inventory process across the bank's locations with an Excel and VBA dashboard, which sped up procurement for new buildings.

Digital Core sits underneath something almost every customer touches daily, where clarity matters more than novelty. That is the kind of work I would like to be part of.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
