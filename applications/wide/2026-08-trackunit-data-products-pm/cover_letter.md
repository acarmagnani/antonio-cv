Dear Hiring Team at Trackunit,

As an architect in Brazil I spent years on the execution of buildings, but what pulled me elsewhere was a different question, not how to build something, but what the data could tell us about what to build and what to improve. Following it led me to an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, focused specifically on data methods for the built environment. Trackunit's Data Products, the Analytics Suite and Benchmarking that turn machine and site data into something an operator can act on, are built around exactly the problem I keep coming back to.

The interesting part of analytics has rarely been the model for me. It is the distance between what a model can output and what a user will actually trust and adopt. In my M.Arch. I built a machine-learning tool that generates development recommendations for buildings in Copenhagen, and spent as much effort on the interface that let different stakeholders adjust the drivers and weighting and read the predictions as on the model itself. Separately, an ensemble price-prediction model I built (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) placed in the top 2% of its competition. Building the analysis is one thing; knowing what makes it usable is the part this role centers on.

The benchmarking side is familiar too. At Ramboll I ran a peer-benchmarking analysis for a corporate client, comparing its practices against industry competitors across several areas to surface gaps and where it stood, and built an automated Excel system that consolidated risk data into a single dashboard that updated dynamically. I also brought AI tools into the research workflow to source and cite evidence at scale, not as a bolt-on but because it genuinely removed friction.

Trackunit is turning construction's machine data into insight that keeps operators on the platform. Doing that well, for data-intensive users, is where I want to take my work next.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
