Dear Hiring Team at Danske Bank,

I trained as an architect, but the part of the work that held me was the step before the drawings, whether a project made financial sense at all. Following that question took me from São Paulo to Copenhagen for an M.Arch in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, where the work was closer to modelling assets, prices and markets than to design. Much of what trades on the Oslo exchange is physical, energy, shipping, industrials, property, and the asset side of that is where I have spent my career so far.

At Ramboll Management Consulting I led an internal market study mapping the global supply chains of more than twenty critical raw materials, profiling producing companies, sectors and geographies from industry databases and connecting regulatory drivers to where the commercial opportunity actually sat. I also ran a peer benchmarking analysis for a corporate client, comparing it against its competitors across service areas to find the gaps and the positioning, and built an automated risk-assessment system in Excel that pulled scattered data into a dashboard that updated itself. Gathering unstructured sector data, forming a view, and then having to defend it in writing was the part of that job I liked most.

Earlier, at OSPA, I produced more than fifteen feasibility studies for developer clients, sizing investment and return against buildable area, unit mix and price per square metre, and presented the result to the client paying for it. On the quantitative side, I finished in the top 2% of a property valuation competition using ensemble models on structured property and financial data, and built an Excel and VBA tool for a private client that ingests monthly statements and turns them into a decision dashboard.

I should be direct about the gap. My finance is applied and self-taught rather than examined, and I do not speak Norwegian, though I work in Danish at a limited level and read the written language. What Danske offers a graduate is coverage of your own, built one company at a time, and that is the kind of work I would give years to.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
