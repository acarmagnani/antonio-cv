Dear Hiring Team at SEB,

I trained as an architect, but the part of the work that held me was always the step before the drawings, deciding whether a project was worth doing and what the numbers had to look like for it to work. That pulled me toward real estate feasibility, then toward data, and eventually from Brazil to Copenhagen for an M.Arch in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, where I spent two years building analytical tools rather than buildings. A portfolio analyst role sitting close to an investment team, where the work is keeping a moving picture accurate and turning it into something a client can read, is where I want that to go next.

At Ramboll I built a fully automated risk-assessment system in Excel for a corporate client, consolidating data scattered across business units and supply chains into a single dashboard that recalculated priorities as the inputs changed. The interesting part was not the formulas. It was that the client had been maintaining the same picture by hand and it was quietly wrong most of the time. I care about the version that stays right without anyone remembering to update it, which is most of what reconciliation and reporting actually is.

Before that, at OSPA, I produced more than fifteen feasibility studies for developers in Brazil and the US, sizing investment and return against buildable area, unit mix and price per square metre, and then presenting those outcomes to the clients paying for them. At Itaú-Unibanco I worked inside a bank, replacing a manual inventory process with a VBA-driven Excel dashboard and reviewing due diligence on buildings the bank was acquiring or leasing.

I have not worked in private equity reporting, and I would be learning fund operations from the beginning. What I bring is a genuinely analytical foundation, Excel as something I build in rather than fill in, and the habit of working independently on things nobody is checking daily. SEB Alternatives spans strategies including real estate, which is the ground I already know.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
