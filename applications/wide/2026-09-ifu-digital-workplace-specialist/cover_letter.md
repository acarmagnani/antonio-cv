Dear Hiring Team at Impact Fund Denmark,

I trained as an architect, and the interest that has run alongside that from the beginning is data - using it to understand what has been built and what could be built better. That is what took me from architecture practice into feasibility and analysis, and then to a Master's Degree in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme built around using data and technology to improve the decisions made in real estate and construction. The through line has been consistent, which is that I would rather build the analytical tool that answers a question than argue the question without one. Impact Fund Denmark is an institution I already know from the delivery side of its capital, since at Sweco I supported water and energy projects financed by IFU alongside the World Bank and the EBRD.

At Isay Weinfeld Architects I wrote a Grasshopper plugin in Python that generates bespoke staircases directly from a floor plan, which took a task that had run to several days down to roughly twenty minutes and made a far wider range of geometry testable within a project timeline. Building it was the smaller half of the work - the rest was sitting with the colleagues who had been doing it by hand to establish what they actually needed, presenting the tool in a studio workshop, and supporting its adoption across more than ten projects afterwards, which taught me that a tool nobody trusts enough to use has not been finished. Training and adoption carry that same weight here, because a global rollout is only complete once colleagues in Nairobi or Sao Paulo use what was deployed rather than work around it.

My work at Sweco extended into the commercial side of the practice, where I wrote the technical and methodology sections of competitive international tenders, assembled bid teams around the specialist expertise each procurement required, and built a pricing tool that parametrised expert fee bands and project economics so that bid decisions rested on figures rather than on judgement alone. Alongside that I joined field missions across Baku's water supply system, where I sat with national water agencies and government counterparts to align on rehabilitation priorities and gather the technical data the project needed. That combination taught me how a consultancy actually wins and then delivers work, and how much of it depends on being precise with people who are not obliged to agree with you. This position asks for the same thing daily, explaining technical matters plainly to colleagues across regional offices and time zones whose priorities differ from my own.

Impact Fund Denmark invests risk capital across more than forty-five countries, run from a Copenhagen office whose Technology and Operations team carries the systems and the security behind that work. That is where I want to keep doing the work I find most worthwhile, which is turning large and awkward bodies of data into something a business can decide on.

Best,
Antonio Carmagnani
