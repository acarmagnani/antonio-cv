Dear Hiring Team at Novo Nordisk,

I trained as an architect in Brazil and kept moving away from the drawing and toward the question that comes before it, which is where the decision actually sits. That took me from São Paulo to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a quantitative programme built on data, modelling and code rather than on design. It also took me into consulting, first at Ramboll and then at Sweco, and what I found there was that the method travels. Structure the problem, make it quantitative, land it somewhere a senior person can act on it. An in-house consultancy reporting to Executive Management is where that has the shortest distance to a decision.

Ramboll Management Consulting is where I learned most of it. I ran a peer benchmarking analysis for a corporate client, comparing its practices against industry competitors across several service areas and turning the gaps into positioning recommendations. I built an impact assessment tool that mapped more than forty salient risks across a client's supply chain and scored them by severity so the material ones surfaced first. And I led an internal market study covering the supply chains of twenty-plus critical raw materials, connecting incoming regulation to concrete business development targets, which is the department-building side of the work your posting asks for.

At Sweco I have been closer to the client's site. I joined a field mission across Baku's water supply system, assessing asset condition and identifying technical, social and environmental risks through inspections and interviews, then sat with national water agencies and government counterparts to align on rehabilitation priorities. Earlier, at an architecture studio in São Paulo, I built a Python tool that cut a modelling task from several days to around twenty minutes, presented it internally, and supported its adoption across more than ten projects. Recommendations only count once someone uses them.

Novo Nordisk Consulting is described as a talent incubator with a steep learning curve. That is the next step I want, and I would arrive with two Danish consultancies' worth of method behind me.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
