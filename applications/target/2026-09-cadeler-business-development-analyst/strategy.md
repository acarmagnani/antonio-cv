# Strategy, Cadeler Business Development Analyst

**Positioning:** An analyst who already does the work this role describes, market mapping to find commercial opportunities, competitor benchmarking, and the trackers and dashboards that carry it to senior management, arriving from the energy and infrastructure side of consulting.

**Profile chosen:** `profile.infrastructure-consulting`. It is the only variant that names energy projects outright, and Cadeler screens for genuine interest in the sector. It also carries the tender-through-delivery arc, which is the commercial half of the job.

**Threads to lead with:**

1. **The Ramboll market study.** He mapped 20+ critical raw material supply chains to identify prospective clients and new service lines, connecting regulatory drivers to concrete business development targets. That is the same motion as reading auction rounds, seabed leasing and government targets for pipeline opportunities. The peer benchmarking analysis sits next to it and covers "market gaps and areas of competitive advantage" almost word for word.

2. **Sweco, energy infrastructure with a commercial edge.** Water, energy and transport assets financed by the World Bank, EBRD, KfW and EIB, where he wrote tender methodology, assembled bid teams, built a pricing tool for bid decisions, and sat with national agencies and government counterparts. Sector fluency plus the bid side of business development.

3. **Tool building as a habit, not a claim.** An automated Excel risk dashboard at Ramboll, a VBA inventory tracker at Itau, a decision dashboard for a private client, Power BI and Python. The posting asks for someone to "build and maintain trackers, dashboards, and reports"; he has done exactly that three times in three different contexts.

**The gap, stated honestly:** no offshore wind, marine or vessel experience, and no prior job titled business analyst or business development. The letter should not apologise for this. It should let the market study and the benchmark carry the "essential" requirement on evidence, and treat the sector as the thing he is moving toward on purpose.
