Dear Hiring Team at European Energy,

I trained as an architect, and the interest that has run alongside that from the beginning is data - using it to understand what has been built and what could be built better. That is what took me from architecture practice into feasibility and analysis, and then to a Master's in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme built around using data and technology to improve the decisions made in real estate and construction. The through line has been consistent, which is that I would rather build the analytical tool that answers a question than argue the question without one. At European Energy that preference has a commercial edge, since developing, financing, building and then selling or co-investing in its own wind, solar and storage projects means the case put to an investor has to hold up against the asset underneath it.

My work at Sweco extended into the commercial side of the practice, where I wrote the technical and methodology sections of competitive international tenders, assembled bid teams around the specialist expertise each procurement required, and built a pricing tool that parametrised expert fee bands and project economics so that bid decisions rested on figures rather than on judgement alone. Alongside that I joined field missions across Baku's water supply system, where I sat with national water agencies and government counterparts to align on rehabilitation priorities and gather the technical data the project needed. That combination taught me how a consultancy actually wins and then delivers work, and how much of it depends on being precise with people who are not obliged to agree with you. Shaping business development proposals for prospective investors alongside Key Account Managers and executive management is that same work in a room where capital rather than a contract is at stake.

At OSPA I produced multiple feasibility studies for residential and mixed-use developments across Brazil and the United States, sizing investment and return for each candidate site against buildable area, unit mix, price per square metre and parking, while reading the municipal master plan and land-use law to establish how much development potential the site actually carried. What that work taught me is that the number a client commits capital against is only as good as the regulatory reading underneath it, and that the two have to be produced together rather than in sequence. A divestment or co-investment case on a wind or solar project turns on the same pairing, because the permitting position, grid connection and offtake terms are what the numbers are allowed to move within.

European Energy owns and operates the assets its investor conversations are about, across more than twenty countries and the full path from development through financing and construction into operation. That is where I want to bring the architect's reading of an asset and the analytical side of my work into the same job, applied to real assets and to decisions with money behind them.

Best,
Antonio Carmagnani
