Dear Hiring Team at DNB,

I trained as an architect, and what progressively drew me beyond the drawings were the questions a design studio rarely stops on - why something is being built, whether it should be built there at all, and what its impact will be over its life. Following those questions took me to an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy and, within it, specifically toward data, which is the part that makes it possible to answer them with evidence instead of intuition, and from there into sustainability and responsible business consulting. DNB is building its ESG data value chains inside Group Risk Management, which is where sustainability regulation stops being a reporting exercise and starts steering a portfolio.

During my time in Ramboll Management Consulting's Responsible Business Conduct team I ran human rights due diligence for corporate clients under the UN Guiding Principles and the OECD Guidelines, and in practice a large part of that was data work. I built a fully automated risk assessment system in Excel that consolidated supply chain risk data into a single dashboard which updated salient risks dynamically, an impact assessment tool that scored more than forty risks by severity so the most material ones surfaced first, and a mapping of the supply chains of over twenty critical raw materials under the EU Critical Raw Materials Act. The common task underneath all of it was taking broad and uneven bodies of evidence and structuring them rigorously enough to produce scored, defensible outputs. That structuring task sits at the centre of this role, since ESG portfolio data arrives from external providers, customer submissions and public registers long before it is reliable enough to report on.

At Sweco I supported advisory teams delivering water, energy and transport projects financed by development banks including the World Bank, EBRD, KfW and EIB, and authored climate risk assessments for major water supply projects under JASPERS and EBRD climate-proofing guidance, screening hazards against projection data and rating exposure and vulnerability asset by asset. Working to lender standards taught me that an assessment is only useful if it survives review by someone with money at stake, which changes how you evidence every claim you make. An independent control function answers to that same standard, because the ESG figures a bank publishes are read by regulators, the Board and the market rather than filed away.

Few institutions put this capability inside the risk function, where the data has to be controlled and the analysis has to hold up under independent review. That is where I want to keep doing the work I find most worthwhile, which is turning large and awkward bodies of data into something a business can decide on.

Best,
Antonio Carmagnani
