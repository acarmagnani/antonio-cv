Dear Hiring Team at McKinsey & Company,

I trained as an architect, and what progressively drew me beyond the drawings were the questions a design studio rarely stops on - why something is being built, whether it should be built there at all, and what its impact will be over its life. Following those questions took me to a Master's Degree in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy and, within it, specifically toward data, which is the part that makes it possible to answer them with evidence instead of intuition, and from there into sustainability and responsible business consulting. Energy Solutions asks those questions of entire energy systems rather than single projects, and a team that turns market data and technology into the analysis clients act on is where I want to be answering them.

At Sweco I supported advisory teams delivering water, energy and transport projects financed by development banks including the World Bank, EBRD, KfW and EIB, and authored climate risk assessments for major water supply projects under JASPERS and EBRD climate-proofing guidance, screening hazards against projection data and rating exposure and vulnerability asset by asset. Working to lender standards taught me that an assessment is only useful if it survives review by someone with money at stake, which changes how you evidence every claim you make. Analysis an energy company or an investor commits to carries that same burden, because the conclusion has to hold in front of people whose capital and operations depend on it.

During my time in Ramboll Management Consulting's Responsible Business Conduct team I ran human rights due diligence for corporate clients under the UN Guiding Principles and the OECD Guidelines, and in practice a large part of that was data work. I built a fully automated risk assessment system in Excel that consolidated supply chain risk data into a single dashboard which updated salient risks dynamically, an impact assessment tool that scored more than forty risks by severity so the most material ones surfaced first, and a mapping of the supply chains of over twenty critical raw materials under the EU Critical Raw Materials Act. The common task underneath all of it was taking broad and uneven bodies of evidence and structuring them rigorously enough to produce scored, defensible outputs. Building the tools and benchmarks that turn uneven market evidence into something a client can decide from is that task again, across the energy value chain and at a far larger scale.

Energy Solutions occupies the position where the energy transition is actually argued, supplying the data and the independent reading of it that operators, investors and governments across the value chain decide from. That is where I want to keep doing the work I find most worthwhile, which is turning large and awkward bodies of data into something a business can decide on.

Best,
Antonio Carmagnani
