# Positioning, McKinsey & Company (Analyst, Energy Solutions, London)

**One line:** an analyst who already works inside international energy and infrastructure consulting, and whose actual craft is building the quantitative tools that turn uneven evidence into something a client decides from.

**Profile chosen:** `profile.infrastructure-consulting`. It is the only variant that names energy projects and international consulting in the opening line, which is the first thing an energy intelligence team looks for, and it lands climate risk assessment and GIS as method rather than as sustainability advocacy.

**Threads to lead with:**

1. **Sweco, energy and infrastructure advisory delivered to lender standard.** Water, energy and transport assets financed by the World Bank, EBRD, KfW and EIB, with climate risk rated asset by asset so the finding drives an investment and adaptation decision, plus the pricing tool that parametrised fee bands and project economics behind bid decisions. The point is not the sustainability content, it is the standard: an assessment that has to survive review by someone with money at stake.
2. **Ramboll, structuring evidence into tools and benchmarks.** The fully automated risk assessment system in Excel with a dashboard that updated dynamically, the peer benchmarking of a client against its industry competitors, the mapping of 20+ critical raw materials supply chains under the EU Critical Raw Materials Act (producing companies, sectors and geographies), and AI research tools integrated into due diligence workflows. The posting's own words are "tools/benchmarks" and "complex models", and this is where he has built them.

**The quantitative spine, carried underneath both:** Excel and VBA (the Ramboll dashboard, the private-client financial tool), Python (the Grasshopper plugin taken up across 10+ projects, the ensemble valuation model in the top 2% of its competition), SQL, Power BI and QGIS. Excel is essential in the posting and Python is a plus, and both are evidenced more than once.

**The decisive gap, named plainly and not papered over:** **no oil and gas, at any point of the value chain.** The posting lists sector experience (upstream, midstream, downstream, refining, petrochemicals, trading) as a qualification rather than a preference, and he has none of it. No energy market modelling either, and no Tableau. The letter does not claim any of it. It leads with analytical method, energy-asset exposure and tool building, which is the only honest case available.

**Realistic read:** a low-probability application to an exceptional platform. The CV is built to be screened on the second qualification (quantitative and modelling ability) because the first one (oil and gas) cannot be met.
