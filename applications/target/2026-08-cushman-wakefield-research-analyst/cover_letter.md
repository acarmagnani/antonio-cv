Dear Hiring Team at Cushman & Wakefield,

I trained as an architect, but what held my attention was never the building itself. It was the layer underneath, the data that explains why a market moves the way it does and what that means for the next decision. That interest took me from real estate feasibility work in Brazil to Copenhagen, where I completed an M.Arch in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy and spent two years building spatial and market models instead of buildings. A research function that exists to turn market data into insight the business can actually sell on is the clearest version of that work I have found.

At Sweco I build the geospatial databases behind internationally financed infrastructure projects, compiling georeferenced asset locations in QGIS from agency records, satellite imagery and open sources, and cleaning them into something the analysis can stand on. I have become the office's cartographic output across several international projects, producing the material that goes into methodology and proposal documents. Most of the work is unglamorous. Data arrives incomplete, inconsistent and in the wrong projection, and the value is in the person who makes it reliable before anyone draws a conclusion from it.

Research that feeds the business is the other half. At Ramboll I led an internal market study mapping more than twenty critical raw material supply chains from authoritative industry databases, profiling producing companies, sectors and geographies, and connected the regulatory drivers behind them to concrete business development targets. I also ran a peer benchmarking analysis comparing a client against its competitors across service areas to identify gaps and positioning. Earlier, at OSPA, I sized development viability against price per square metre, unit mix and buildable area across Brazilian and US markets, then produced the diagrams and presented the findings to the developers paying for them.

I work across markets and languages, currently in Denmark and in English, and I am used to being the person joining data from several countries into one readable picture. C&W's research platform across EMEA is where I would like to do that properly.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
