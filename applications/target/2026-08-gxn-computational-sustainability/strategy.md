### Strategy: GXN Innovation — unsolicited application (Copenhagen)

**Positioning (one line)**
An architect who builds the computational tools a design practice runs on, and who does the
environmental analysis quantitatively (LCA, embodied carbon, material flows) rather than
rhetorically. The rare candidate who has done both inside real practice and inside research.

**Throughline for the letter**
"Tools that change what a practice can afford to try." Every paragraph points back to Antonio
building something (a plugin, a toolkit, a material system, a dashboard) that let other people
make a better decision. This matches GXN's actual identity: a research unit attached to a
practice that builds.

**Profile chosen**
`[proptech]` variant, verbatim. It names computational design, academic research AND architecture
practice, Grasshopper, Python, QGIS, ML. See the gap note below.

**The hook (this is the whole argument of the letter)**
Antonio studied at CITA, the department at The Royal Danish Academy with a standing relationship
to GXN. Through it he: (1) took part in a workshop with GXN and spent a week working in GXN's
office, where he produced the geospatial material-source and supply-flow mappings for the
Matanzas project; (2) developed the rammed blue biomass composite that became the material
foundation for the Venice Biennale 2025 installation presented with CITA, GXN and 3XN; (3) had
recurring contact with GXN people who sat on juries at school deliveries and ran smaller
workshops. He knows the company from the inside and liked what he saw. This is not manufactured
enthusiasm, and it is what makes an unsolicited letter land.

**Why GXN specifically (the emotional core)**
GXN works with data and computation as a normal part of building. That is exactly what Antonio
found missing in the architecture offices he worked in, and it is the reason he left execution
work in the first place. The letter should make that the pivot: the thing he went looking for is
the thing GXN already does.

**Threads to lead with**
1. **Isay Weinfeld (anchor).** The Grasshopper/Python staircase plugin: days to ~20 minutes,
   presented in a workshop, adopted across 10+ projects. Proves he builds tools for designers and
   gets them adopted, which is GXN's core activity.
2. **The material/environmental spine.** Blue biomass + Venice, the Revit embodied carbon toolkit
   (GWP A1-A3, A4), Ladybug daylight simulation. Covers GXN's LCA, material research and
   circularity work.
3. **Quantitative rigor from consulting.** Ramboll critical raw materials supply chain mapping
   (materials + flows, bridges to material passports), the EU building performance / decarbonization
   service overview, and Sweco climate risk. Shows the analysis holds up outside academia.
