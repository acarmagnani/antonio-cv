Dear Hiring Team at Heitman,

I trained as an architect in Brazil, and what kept pulling at me was never the drawing but the question underneath it, what a building is actually worth and what could go wrong with it over the years someone holds it. That moved me out of design and into feasibility, valuation and risk, and it is why I left São Paulo for Copenhagen and an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme about using data to make better decisions on real estate. Heitman has been publishing with ULI since 2019 on how investors price climate risk into those decisions, which is an unusual thing for an investment manager to do and the reason this role caught me.

At OSPA I produced more than fifteen feasibility studies for residential and mixed-use developments across Brazil and the United States, sizing investment and return against buildable area, unit mix and price per square metre, and reading master plans and land-use law to find where value was actually available. At Felipe Hess Architects I ran the financial feasibility alongside the regulatory work, testing saleable area and development cost against what each site could legally hold. That is the number a business plan is later measured against, and building it is most of what I have done.

At Sweco I assessed physical climate risk across major water infrastructure assets under JASPERS and EBRD climate-proofing standards, rating each asset by exposure and vulnerability to inform adaptation and investment decisions. At Ramboll I developed the firm's real estate ESG due diligence offering around building energy and decarbonisation under EU building performance regulation, and reviewed the Code of Conduct of real estate investment managers against international standards. On an operational portfolio, that work belongs inside the business plan rather than beside it.

I want to be on the side of the table that owns the asset and lives with the hold period. Heitman's European alternatives portfolio is where I would want to learn how that is done.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
