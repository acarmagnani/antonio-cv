Dear Hiring Team at CBRE,

I trained as an architect in Brazil, and the part of the work that held me was never the drawing. It was the two questions that come before it and after it. What does this asset cost, and what does it consume. Most of my career has been spent on one side or the other, and I moved from Brazil to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation because I wanted the data to answer both at once. CBRE, and Global Workplace Solutions in particular, is one of the few places where those two questions belong to the same job.

The financial side came first. At OSPA I produced more than fifteen feasibility studies for mixed-use and residential developments across Brazil and the United States, sizing investment and return against buildable area, unit mix and price per square metre, and reading municipal master plans to work out what a site was actually allowed to become. At Itaú-Unibanco I sat on the other end of that, assessing the environmental and energy performance of buildings the bank was acquiring or leasing by going through consumption data, efficiency ratings and certifications before a decision was signed off.

The environmental side is where I have spent the last two years. At Ramboll I put together the firm's real estate ESG due diligence offering, centred on building energy and decarbonisation under EU building performance regulation, and reviewed the code of conduct of real estate investment managers against international standards. At Sweco I authored climate risk assessments for major water supply projects under JASPERS and EBRD guidance, rating risk by exposure and vulnerability asset by asset. Alongside both, I keep building the tools. A Python plugin that cut a modelling task from days to twenty minutes, an Excel system that pulled scattered risk data into one dashboard, a machine learning model for property valuation that finished in the top two percent of its competition.

I want to work where buildings are measured properly, by people who own the consequences of the measurement. That is CBRE.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
