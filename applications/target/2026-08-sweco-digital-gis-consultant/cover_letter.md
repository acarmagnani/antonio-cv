Dear Hiring Team at Sweco Digital,

Until July I worked at Sweco Danmark, in the infrastructure division, where I ended up as the office's GIS lead across projects. That is the short reason I am writing. The longer one is that I trained as an architect in Brazil, moved to Copenhagen for an M.Arch in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, and have spent the years since turning spatial data into things people can actually use. A team whose whole subject is GIS, built with customers rather than for them, is where I want that to go next.

At Sweco I built geospatial databases for water and infrastructure projects in QGIS, compiling georeferenced asset data, satellite imagery and open sources into something the technical teams could analyse, and produced the cartographic material locating infrastructure across several international projects for methodology and proposal documents. On a field mission across Baku's water supply system I sat with national water agencies and government counterparts to align on rehabilitation priorities and collect the technical and geographic data the project ran on. Data is easiest to model and hardest to get, and that trip taught me the difference.

The consulting half comes from elsewhere. For two years I advised a software startup in the built environment on product direction, UI and UX, testing releases and handing back improved Revit and Dynamo scripts rather than only opinions. At Isay Weinfeld I wrote a Grasshopper plugin in Python, then ran a studio workshop and supported colleagues adopting it across more than ten projects, which is where I learned that translating between specialists and users is slower and more interesting than the code. My master's thesis was a computational analysis of central São Paulo, integrating geospatial datasets in QGIS at neighbourhood scale.

Spatial Suite serves 40+ customers, which means the work is as much about how people use a platform as about the geometry underneath it. That combination is exactly what I want to work on.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
