Dear Hiring Team at CBRE,

I trained as an architect, and what pulled me away from drawing buildings was wanting to know how they actually perform once they are running. A design intent is a claim. Consumption data, efficiency ratings and a certificate are the check on that claim, and the gap between the two is where the interesting work sits. That is what took me from Brazil to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation, and it is why an energy and sustainability role inside CBRE's workplace solutions business interests me.

At Sweco I authored climate risk assessments for major water supply projects, following JASPERS and EBRD climate-proofing guidance. That meant screening hazards against projections data, rating risk by exposure and vulnerability asset by asset, and writing it up so that a client and a lender could both review the reasoning and disagree with it if they wanted to. Working inside someone else's framework, where the method is fixed and your job is to apply it consistently and defend every rating, is the part I found genuinely satisfying. I understand that ISO 14001 and 50001 work has the same shape, though I have not yet worked to those two standards.

The building side started earlier. At Itaú-Unibanco I assessed the environmental and energy performance of buildings the bank was acquiring or leasing, going through consumption data, efficiency ratings and certifications, and I built a VBA dashboard that replaced a manual tracking process the procurement team had been running by hand. At Ramboll I put together the firm's real estate ESG due diligence offering, centred on building energy and decarbonisation under EU building performance regulation, and a separate automated system in Excel that pulled scattered risk data into one dashboard that updated on its own.

CBRE sits closer to operating assets than any consultancy I have worked in, which is the direction I want to move. Reporting on a building you are also responsible for running is a sharper version of the same question I have been chasing.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
