Dear Hiring Team at EY,

I trained as an architect, and what progressively drew me beyond the drawings were the questions a design studio rarely stops on - why something is being built, whether it should be built there at all, and what its impact will be over its life. Following those questions took me to a Master's Degree in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy and, within it, specifically toward data, which is the part that makes it possible to answer them with evidence instead of intuition, and from there into sustainability and responsible business consulting. EY's specialist group in Stockholm works both sides of the same requirement, reviewing sustainability reports and advising on how they are produced, at the point where CSRD has put sustainability information under something close to the evidentiary standard of the financial statements it now sits beside.

During my time in Ramboll Management Consulting's Responsible Business Conduct team I ran human rights due diligence for corporate clients under the UN Guiding Principles and the OECD Guidelines, and in practice a large part of that was data work. I built a fully automated risk assessment system in Excel that consolidated supply chain risk data into a single dashboard which updated salient risks dynamically, an impact assessment tool that scored more than forty risks by severity so the most material ones surfaced first, and a mapping of the supply chains of over twenty critical raw materials under the EU Critical Raw Materials Act. The common task underneath all of it was taking broad and uneven bodies of evidence and structuring them rigorously enough to produce scored, defensible outputs. Reporting rests on that same task, since a sustainability statement is only as reliable as the evidence trail behind each figure a company puts its name to.

At Sweco I supported advisory teams delivering water, energy and transport projects financed by development banks including the World Bank, EBRD, KfW and EIB, and authored climate risk assessments for major water supply projects under JASPERS and EBRD climate-proofing guidance, screening hazards against projection data and rating exposure and vulnerability asset by asset. Working to lender standards taught me that an assessment is only useful if it survives review by someone with money at stake, which changes how you evidence every claim you make. Assurance work answers to that standard by design, because the information reviewed in this role is read by owners, investors and other stakeholders who then act on it.

EY audits this reporting and advises on it across large listed companies and smaller ones at the same time, which gives an unusually direct view of how sustainability information is actually produced and where it turns out not to hold. That is where I want to keep building sustainability work that is measurable, backed by evidence, and able to withstand scrutiny.

Best,
Antonio Carmagnani
