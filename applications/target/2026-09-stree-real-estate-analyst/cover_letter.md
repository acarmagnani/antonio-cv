Dear Hiring Team at STREE Real Estate,

I trained as an architect, and the part of the work that has always drawn me most is the one that comes before the project itself - understanding what a site is worth and what it could become once its regulatory and physical constraints are properly read. Following that question is what took me from architecture practice into development feasibility, and then to a Master's Degree in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, where I specialised in the computational and data methods that allow those questions to be answered with evidence rather than intuition. Alongside that, the environmental performance of buildings has been a constant thread in my work, from assessing the energy performance of assets a bank was acquiring to ESG due diligence for real estate investment managers, which is increasingly part of what an asset is worth rather than a separate conversation about it. The analyst position at STREE Real Estate holds all of that in one seat, where market analysis, valuation and acquisition due diligence feed the same investment decisions.

At OSPA I produced multiple feasibility studies for residential and mixed-use developments across Brazil and the United States, sizing investment and return for each candidate site against buildable area, unit mix, price per square metre and parking, while reading the municipal master plan and land-use law to establish how much development potential the site actually carried. What that work taught me is that the number a client commits capital against is only as good as the regulatory reading underneath it, and that the two have to be produced together rather than in sequence. Analysing a property's financials, and supporting the due diligence behind acquiring it, is that same exercise on an asset that already exists, with the same standard applied to the number.

My Master's Degree in Architecture Computation gave the analytical side of my work its proper depth. I built a predictive property valuation model using ensemble learning across XGBoost, LightGBM, Ridge and CatBoost that finished in the top two per cent of its competition, and a machine learning tool that generates site-specific development recommendations for buildings in Copenhagen by combining financial, social, sustainability and geospatial data, with an interface that lets non-technical stakeholders adjust the impact drivers and weightings themselves. The second of those mattered more to me than the first, because it forced the modelling to survive contact with people who would never open the code. Analysing market data and reading where a market is moving is that work under its commercial name, and I have built both halves, the model and the interface a decision-maker can interrogate.

STREE Real Estate places that analysis where it carries weight, behind what the firm buys and what it builds. That is where I want to bring the architect's reading of an asset and the analytical side of my work into the same job, applied to real assets and to decisions with money behind them.

Best,
Antonio Carmagnani
