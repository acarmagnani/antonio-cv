Dear Hiring Team at European Energy,

I trained as an architect, and the interest that has run alongside that from the beginning is data - using it to understand what has been built and what could be built better. That is what took me from architecture practice into feasibility and analysis, and then to a Master's in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme built around using data and technology to improve the decisions made in real estate and construction. The through line has been consistent, which is that I would rather build the analytical tool that answers a question than argue the question without one. European Energy is the kind of place where that preference carries direct financial consequence, since a company that finances, builds and operates its own solar, wind and Power-to-X projects has to establish what an asset is worth and how much debt it can carry long before anything is constructed, and the Project and Asset Finance team is the part of the business that produces those figures.

At Sweco I supported advisory teams delivering water, energy and transport projects financed by development banks including the World Bank, EBRD, KfW and EIB, and authored climate risk assessments for major water supply projects under JASPERS and EBRD climate-proofing guidance, screening hazards against projection data and rating exposure and vulnerability asset by asset. Working to lender standards taught me that an assessment is only useful if it survives review by someone with money at stake, which changes how you evidence every claim you make. That standard is the one this role works to daily, because maintaining a dataroom and coordinating lender and advisor questions means every figure has to hold up in front of the institutions putting up the money.

At OSPA I produced multiple feasibility studies for residential and mixed-use developments across Brazil and the United States, sizing investment and return for each candidate site against buildable area, unit mix, price per square metre and parking, while reading the municipal master plan and land-use law to establish how much development potential the site actually carried. What that work taught me is that the number a client commits capital against is only as good as the regulatory reading underneath it, and that the two have to be produced together rather than in sequence. Running scenarios and sensitivities on a project finance model asks for that same discipline on a different asset class, where the technical, regulatory and grid conditions of a wind or solar project set the boundaries within which the numbers are allowed to move.

European Energy develops, finances, constructs and operates renewable assets across more than twenty countries, which means its financing team works on projects the company will own and run rather than on transactions that end at signing. That is where I want to bring the architect's reading of an asset and the analytical side of my work into the same job, applied to real assets and to decisions with money behind them.

Best,
Antonio Carmagnani
