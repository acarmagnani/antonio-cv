Dear Hiring Team at Urban Partners,

I trained as an architect, and the question that pulled me away from the drawing was always the same one. Does this actually work. Not aesthetically. Whether the site, the rules and the numbers add up to something worth building, and who is willing to put money behind it. Most of my career has been spent on the developer's side of that question. The Credit team's side, deciding what is worth financing and on what terms, is where I want to take it next.

At OSPA I produced more than fifteen feasibility studies for mixed-use and residential developments across Brazil and the United States. The work was sizing investment and return against buildable area, unit mix, price per square metre and parking, then reading municipal master plans closely enough to find where the regulations had left buildable value unclaimed. Each study either concluded that a site cleared its threshold or that it did not, and a developer made a real decision on the strength of it.

The quantitative side has kept developing since. I built a real estate valuation model using ensemble learning on structured property and financial data, which finished in the top two percent of its competition, and a machine learning tool that generates development recommendations for buildings from financial, social and geospatial inputs. At Sweco I developed a pricing tool that parametrised project economics to test the viability of different bid configurations, and wrote the climate risk chapters that went to clients and lenders on internationally financed projects. At Ramboll I worked on the real estate side of ESG, including a code of conduct review for real estate investment managers.

I have not underwritten credit, and I am not going to pretend that term sheets and loan documentation are familiar ground. What I bring is several years of judging whether a real estate project holds up under its own numbers, and the analytical habits to pick up the rest quickly. Urban Partners finances the assets that decide how cities actually get built, and that is the work I want to be close to.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
