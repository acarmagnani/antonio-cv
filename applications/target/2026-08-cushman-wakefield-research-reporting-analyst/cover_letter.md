Dear Hiring Team at Cushman & Wakefield,

I trained as an architect in Brazil, but the part of the work that held me was the step before the drawings, deciding where something should go and whether the numbers support it. That pulled me toward data and toward markets, and eventually from São Paulo to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme built around using data to make better decisions about real estate. Insights & Analytics is where that question gets asked at portfolio scale, across a client's whole footprint rather than one site.

At Ramboll I did the research half of this job for corporate clients. I ran a peer benchmarking analysis comparing a client's practices against its industry competitors across several service areas, identifying gaps, competitive advantages and places where its positioning could be strengthened. I also led an internal market study mapping the supply chains of more than twenty critical raw materials, connecting regulatory drivers to concrete business development targets. Both ended the same way, with a document a senior audience read and acted on, which is the part of research I care about.

The modelling comes from the real estate side. At OSPA I produced more than fifteen feasibility studies for developments in Brazil and the US, sizing investment and return against buildable area, unit mix and price per square metre, then presented the outcomes to developer clients. I have built three analytical tools in Excel for three employers, including a fully automated risk-assessment system that consolidated scattered data into one dynamic dashboard, and a VBA dashboard tracking a bank's corporate real estate portfolio across locations. My master's thesis was a computational analysis of central São Paulo, combining urban morphology, land use and social vulnerability data to find underused space, which is location analysis by another name.

Cushman & Wakefield's Insights & Analytics team sits where the data actually reaches the decision, at the scale of a whole portfolio. That is the work I want to be doing next.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
