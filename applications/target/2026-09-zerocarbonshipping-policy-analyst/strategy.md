# Maersk Mc-Kinney Moller Center for Zero Carbon Shipping, Analyst in Economics and Transition Finance

## Positioning

The team's analyst, not a sustainability advisor. The whole CV has to answer one question, which is whether he can build a model, defend its assumptions, handle a messy dataset and write the result up for someone who will never open the code. Climate and regulation are the domain he already works in, so they are the spine; the delta makes the quantitative half of his record the thing that shows.

## The two threads to lead with

1. **Climate risk assessment to lender standards.** At Sweco he authored climate risk assessments for water supply projects under JASPERS and EBRD climate-proofing guidance, screening hazards against projection data and rating exposure and vulnerability asset by asset, on projects financed by the World Bank, EBRD, KfW and EIB. That is regulatory impact assessment in everything but name, and it was produced for readers who had money at stake, which is the closest analogue to writing for regulators and IMO delegations.

2. **Models and tools he built himself.** The ensemble-learning valuation model in the top 2% of its competition, the machine learning recommendation tool with an interface that lets non-technical stakeholders move the weightings themselves, the automated Excel risk system at Ramboll feeding a dashboard that updates dynamically, the pricing tool at Sweco that parametrised project economics. Between them they cover Python, advanced Excel, scenarios, and dashboards for non-technical users, which are four separate lines in the posting.

The posting asks for those as advantages rather than requirements, but they are what a stronger candidate will have, and they are where an interview will push.
