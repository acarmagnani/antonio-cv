Dear Hiring Team at EY-Parthenon,

I trained as an architect in Brazil, and the part that held me was never the drawing. It was the question underneath it, whether a site was worth what someone was about to pay for it, and what in the data was actually driving that number. Following that question upstream is what brought me to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme built around using data to make better decisions in real estate and construction. A Data & Analytics team running that same method on transactions, across the Nordics, is where I want to take it next.

The commercial half is not new to me. At OSPA I produced more than 15 feasibility studies for developers in São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Porto Alegre and Miami, sizing investment and return against buildable area, unit mix, price per square metre and parking. Each one began as unstructured material, zoning law, market comparables, site constraints, and had to end as a number a client could commit capital against. I also co-founded and ran a small studio serving more than ten clients, handling my own scoping, pricing and hiring, which is how I learned what a business looks like from the inside of its margins.

The technical half has run alongside it. I built a property valuation model using XGBoost, LightGBM, Ridge and CatBoost that finished in the top 2% of its competition, and a machine learning tool that merges financial, social, sustainability and geospatial data into site-specific development recommendations for Copenhagen, with an interface non-technical stakeholders drive themselves. At Ramboll I put AI research tools into our due diligence workflow to source and cite risk data across volumes we could not have covered by hand.

I am not a finance graduate, and the transaction side is something I would be learning from the ground. A team assembled out of data science, engineering, finance and accounting looks like one that hires for what a person can do rather than for what their degree says, and Copenhagen is already home.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
