Dear Hiring Team at Eidra,

I trained as an architect in Brazil, and then spent the following years quietly leaving architecture. Not out of boredom, but because the questions I cared about kept sitting one step upstream of the drawing. Why here, on what evidence, and what would a better version cost. Chasing that took me into feasibility studies for developers, then to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, where I learned to build the analytical tools rather than ask someone else for them, and from there into sustainability consulting and international infrastructure advisory. Your programme offers twelve months of moving between companies to find where a person is actually useful. I have been running a less organised version of that on my own.

The consulting part is already real. At Ramboll Management Consulting I built an automated risk assessment in Excel that pulled supply chain data into a single dashboard and reprioritised as the inputs changed, and I benchmarked a client against its competitors to find where it was exposed and where it could push. At Sweco I wrote the methodology sections of competitive international tenders, built a pricing tool that put bid decisions on numbers, and sat with national water agencies in Baku to agree rehabilitation priorities and gather data nobody had assembled. Real clients, real deadlines, real consequences for being wrong.

Design and analysis have never been separate tracks for me. I built a property valuation model using XGBoost, LightGBM, Ridge and CatBoost that finished in the top 2% of its competition. I also developed a construction technique made from seashells and algae, which was selected as a semester highlight and became the material basis for my school's installation at the Venice Architecture Biennale, shown at the Arsenale with CITA, GXN and 3XN.

A collective that keeps creative studios and strategy firms in the same house is an unusual thing, and it is the reason I am writing. I would like a year to find out which of those rooms I am most useful in.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
