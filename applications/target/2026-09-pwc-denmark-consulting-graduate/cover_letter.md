Dear Hiring Team at PwC,

I trained as an architect, and what progressively drew me beyond the drawings were the questions a design studio rarely stops on - why something is being built, whether it should be built there at all, and what its impact will be over its life. Following those questions took me to a Master's Degree in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy and, within it, specifically toward data, which is the part that makes it possible to answer them with evidence instead of intuition, and from there into sustainability and responsible business consulting. Strategy & Transformation Excellence is the track I am applying to at PwC, because strategy carried through into the transformation itself, with energy-sector specialists inside the same team, is the closest thing to the projects I have already worked on.

My work at Sweco extended into the commercial side of the practice, where I wrote the technical and methodology sections of competitive international tenders, assembled bid teams around the specialist expertise each procurement required, and built a pricing tool that parametrised expert fee bands and project economics so that bid decisions rested on figures rather than on judgement alone. Alongside that I joined field missions across Baku's water supply system, where I sat with national water agencies and government counterparts to align on rehabilitation priorities and gather the technical data the project needed. That combination taught me how a consultancy actually wins and then delivers work, and how much of it depends on being precise with people who are not obliged to agree with you. That is the consulting day described in this programme, where the work runs from analysing the problem to facilitating the room in which it gets decided.

During my time in Ramboll Management Consulting's Responsible Business Conduct team I ran human rights due diligence for corporate clients under the UN Guiding Principles and the OECD Guidelines, and in practice a large part of that was data work. I built a fully automated risk assessment system in Excel that consolidated supply chain risk data into a single dashboard which updated salient risks dynamically, an impact assessment tool that scored more than forty risks by severity so the most material ones surfaced first, and a mapping of the supply chains of over twenty critical raw materials under the EU Critical Raw Materials Act. The common task underneath all of it was taking broad and uneven bodies of evidence and structuring them rigorously enough to produce scored, defensible outputs. Client problems arrive in exactly that condition, and the value of a consultant lies in the step between the evidence and the decision.

PwC advises Danish companies and public organisations across strategy, operations and technology at a scale few firms here reach, and Hellerup keeps that work in the city where I have built my professional life. That is where I want to keep doing the work I find most worthwhile, which is turning large and awkward bodies of data into something a business can decide on.

Best,
Antonio Carmagnani
