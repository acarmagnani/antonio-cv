Dear Hiring Team at VELUX,

I trained and worked as an architect, and what pulled me out of the studio was the question studios rarely get to answer, why the industry builds the way it does and what would actually make it change. Following that took me from São Paulo to Copenhagen for an M.Arch in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, and then into consulting, where I have spent my time building the analysis behind decisions. Working with architects and building designers, from the inside of a company whose product is daylight, is a version of that I would take seriously.

The clearest example is small. At Isay Weinfeld I wrote a Grasshopper plugin in Python that generates bespoke staircases from a floor plan, cutting the modelling from days to around twenty minutes. Building it was the easy part. I then presented it in a studio workshop and supported colleagues through adopting it across more than ten projects, which meant understanding how each of them worked, where the tool got in the way, and what would make somebody change a habit they were comfortable with. Convincing designers is not a communication exercise, it is a design problem in itself.

The analytical half comes from consulting. At Ramboll I led an internal market study mapping the supply chains of more than twenty critical raw materials, connecting regulatory drivers to concrete business development targets, and I developed the firm's real estate ESG due diligence service from proposition to client-facing material. At Sweco I have written the technical and methodology sections of international tender proposals and built the pricing tool behind bid decisions. I also spent years running daylight and solar-exposure simulations to drive massing and orientation, quantifying light incidence across layouts, so daylight has been a number to me as much as an argument.

VELUX works on the part of a building people actually feel, and does it as a company that reinvests what it earns. That is a good place to put this next.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
