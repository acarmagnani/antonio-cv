Dear Hiring Team at Constanter,

I trained as an architect, but what held my attention was the step before the drawings, deciding whether an asset is worth the capital and what it could become. That pulled me toward the numbers underneath buildings, and two long-standing interests came with me, data and environmental performance. A Real Assets desk running across property, infrastructure and natural resources, inside an endowment that invests in perpetuity and treats sustainability as part of the return rather than beside it, is where those three finally sit in one job.

At Sweco I supported advisory teams on capital-intensive water, energy and transport assets financed by the World Bank, EBRD, KfW and EIB, working to the appraisal and reporting standards those lenders require. I assessed physical climate risk across major water infrastructure assets under JASPERS and EBRD climate-proofing guidance, rating each one by exposure and vulnerability to inform adaptation and investment decisions, and I built a pricing tool that parametrised fee bands and project economics to support bid decisions. On a field mission across Baku's water supply system I assessed asset condition on site and engaged the national water agencies to align on rehabilitation priorities. The habit that came out of it is judging an asset by walking it and then defending the numbers afterwards.

The property half came first. At OSPA I produced more than fifteen feasibility studies, sizing investment and return against buildable area, unit mix and price per square metre, and at Itaú-Unibanco I assessed the energy and environmental performance of buildings the bank was acquiring or leasing. At Ramboll I conducted due diligence for real estate investment clients, reviewed the codes of conduct of real estate investment managers against international standards, and built an automated risk-assessment system in Excel that consolidated the data into a single live dashboard. I work in Python too, including a property valuation model that finished in the top 2% of its competition.

I hold Italian citizenship and would relocate to Zug. Constanter is an unusual place to learn underwriting, because the mandate is perpetual and the sustainability question is already in the room.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
