Dear Hiring Team at KPMG,

I trained as an architect in Brazil, and what pulled me out of practice is the same thing that eventually brought me into sustainability work. Architecture stops at delivery, and the questions I cared about sat upstream of it. Why this, at what cost and to whom, and what would make the decision defensible. That took me from São Paulo to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, built around data-driven approaches to sustainability, and from there into consulting. Seeing human rights and sustainable supply chains listed next to climate change adaptation on your service list is unusual, because those are the two things I have spent the last two years on.

At Ramboll Management Consulting I conducted human rights due diligence for corporate clients, applying the UN Guiding Principles and the OECD Guidelines to assess salient risks across global supply chains. I built a Human Rights Impact Assessment tool mapping more than forty salient risks with severity scoring, and a salience-based due diligence system scoring risks across business units by probability, scale and scope so that the material ones surfaced automatically. I also ran a peer benchmarking analysis comparing a client's responsible business practices against its competitors, and produced the workshop materials that took those findings back to them.

At Sweco I moved to the climate side, authoring climate risk assessments for major water supply projects under JASPERS and EBRD climate-proofing guidance, screening hazards against projections data and rating each asset by exposure and vulnerability. A field mission across Baku's water supply system meant assessing asset condition and gathering risk data through inspections and interviews rather than from a desk.

The digital half is not a claim. I built that risk tooling myself in Excel, integrated AI research tools into our due diligence workflows to source and cite evidence at volume, and work in Python and QGIS.

I speak four languages fluently and some Danish, which makes Swedish a shorter step than a new one. A sustainability team that sits inside a broader consulting practice rather than beside it is where I want to build this.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
