Dear Hiring Team at Greystar,

I trained as an architect, but the part of the work that drew me most was the step before the drawings, deciding whether a site was worth acquiring and what it could realistically become. That question is what pulled me into real estate feasibility early on, and then toward data, and eventually from Brazil to Copenhagen for an M.Arch in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, where I spent two years on financial, spatial and market modelling of buildings. A development analyst role on a residential platform being built out across Europe is where I want to take that.

At OSPA I produced more than fifteen feasibility studies for developer clients across Brazil and the US, in São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Porto Alegre and Miami. For each site I sized investment and return against buildable area, unit mix, price per square metre and parking, and worked through the municipal master plan and land-use law to establish what the site actually permitted, including where the regulatory room to increase buildable value was. Then I presented the result to the client paying for it. Reading a site's constraints, turning them into numbers and landing on a recommendation somebody can act on is the work I want to keep doing.

The data side runs alongside it. For my master's I built a machine learning tool that generates site-specific development recommendations for buildings in Copenhagen, integrating financial, social, sustainability and geospatial data, with an interface letting different stakeholders adjust the weighting. I also finished in the top 2% of a valuation modelling competition using ensemble learning on property and financial data. Earlier, at Itaú-Unibanco, I ran due diligence on the environmental and energy performance of buildings the bank was acquiring or leasing, and reviewed technical documentation against building, accessibility and fire-safety norms.

Greystar is assembling a European build-to-rent and student housing platform at a scale where the underwriting genuinely decides things, and residential is the asset class I know best. That is the work I left the drawing board to do.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
