Dear Hiring Team at Ardian,

I trained as an architect, but the part of the work that held me was the step before the drawings, deciding whether a site is worth buying and what it could become. That pulled me away from design and toward the numbers underneath it, and two long-standing interests came with me, data and the environmental performance of buildings. Both now sit inside how real estate is valued and managed. This role in Frankfurt, where underwriting an acquisition and then carrying the refurbishment business plan through on the asset are the same job, is where I want to take that further.

At OSPA in São Paulo I produced more than fifteen feasibility studies for mixed-use and residential developments across Brazil and the United States, sizing investment and return on each site against buildable area, unit mix, price per square metre and parking, and reading the municipal master plan to find where regulation had left value on the table. I presented the outcomes to the developer clients who had to act on them. The work taught me to take an asset's constraints, turn them into numbers, and land on a recommendation someone can commit capital to.

The asset side of that came from two directions. At Ramboll I conducted due diligence for real estate investment clients and reviewed the codes of conduct of real estate investment managers against international standards, and at Itaú-Unibanco I assessed the energy and environmental performance of buildings the bank was acquiring or leasing. I also tend to build the tools I work with, an Excel and VBA model that ingests monthly statements and sorts them into a decision dashboard, and a property valuation model in Python that finished in the top 2% of its competition.

Ardian invests at a scale where the analysis has to hold up in front of an investment committee and then survive contact with the building. That pairing is what I moved from Brazil to Denmark to work on.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
