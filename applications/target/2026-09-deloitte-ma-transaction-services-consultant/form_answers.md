## 1. Please motivate why you are applying to Deloitte

I have spent the last two years inside large advisory firms, first at Ramboll Management Consulting and then at Sweco, and what that taught me is that the size of the firm is what makes the work interesting rather than what makes it distant. At Ramboll I ran due diligence for corporate and real estate investment clients, and at Sweco I supported advisory teams working to the appraisal and reporting standards that development bank lenders require, which meant every claim had to survive review by someone with money at stake. Deloitte is that same discipline at a different order of magnitude, advising private equity firms and strategic corporates across industries and borders, with the sector specialists and the research to take on questions a smaller firm would have to decline. The breadth matters to me for a second reason, which is that Transaction Services sits alongside valuation, real estate advisory and sustainability practices, and the questions I find most worthwhile tend to sit between them. I am also applying for the structure a firm of this size can offer, meaning real engagements early, coaching from colleagues who have run many more processes than I have, and a clear standard of what good analytical work looks like.

## 2. Please motivate why you are applying to this position

Due diligence is work I have done repeatedly, approached from the asset side, and this position is where that same discipline gets applied to whole companies. At Itaú-Unibanco I assessed the environmental and energy performance of buildings the bank was acquiring or leasing as part of internal due diligence, which put me on the owner's side of a purchase and taught me that an institution needs a narrower and harder answer than a design team does. At Ramboll I ran due diligence for corporate and real estate investment clients under the UN Guiding Principles and the OECD Guidelines, and reviewed a real estate investment manager's Code of Conduct against international standards to find where it left the firm exposed. At Sweco I worked on capital-intensive assets financed by the World Bank, the EBRD and the EIB, where the reporting standard is set by the lender rather than by the client. What draws me to this role specifically is that the deliverable is a document a buyer or a seller will act on, so reviewing historical trading, testing whether a forecast is consistent with it, and writing that up so it holds under challenge is analysis with a decision at the end of it. Working both buy side and sell side is also the fastest way I can think of to learn how businesses actually behave.

## 3. How would you describe your interest in and experience with Financial Due Diligence?

My experience is in the financial analysis of real assets rather than of companies, and OSPA is where it is most concentrated. As a trainee in real estate product development I produced more than fifteen feasibility studies for residential and mixed-use schemes in Brazil and the United States, sizing investment and return for each candidate site against buildable area, unit mix, price per square metre and parking, while reading the municipal master plan and land use law to establish how much the site could actually carry. What stayed with me is that the number a client commits capital against is only as good as the assumptions underneath it, and that most of the work is testing whether those assumptions survive contact with the regulation and the market rather than producing the model itself. I did the same at Felipe Hess Architects, testing saleable area, unit mix and development cost against the buildable programme. Alongside that I have built the tooling this kind of analysis runs on, including an automated risk assessment system in Excel at Ramboll that consolidated supply chain data into a single dashboard, and an Excel and VBA tool that ingests monthly statements and PDF exports, categorises transactions and renders them into a decision dashboard. What I want from this role is to carry that discipline from assets to companies.
