# Positioning, Hines (Construction Data & Research Analyst, Conceptual Construction Group, Europe)

**One line:** someone who has built the analytical tools and the databases on both sides of this job, the construction project side and the data side, and who has already had to persuade project teams to actually use what he built.

**Profile chosen:** `profile.proptech-real-estate`. It is the only variant that opens on real estate development and computational work in the same sentence, names financial feasibility modelling as a hands-on skill, and closes on "real estate, technology, and data", which is the intersection this seat sits at. The pure proptech variant understates the development half, and the investment variant understates the data half.

**Threads to lead with:**

1. **Building the tool and getting it adopted.** The Grasshopper plugin in Python that cut staircase modelling from days to twenty minutes, presented in a studio workshop and taken up across 10+ projects. This posting's first responsibility is driving construction data out of every European project across a large concurrent portfolio, and it asks for diplomacy and influencing skills in the same breath. That is a people problem before it is a database problem, and it is the part of his record most candidates will have nothing to say about.
2. **The analytical machinery, already built.** Geospatial databases in QGIS at Sweco, the fully automated Excel risk dashboard at Ramboll, AI research tools integrated into due diligence workflows, the mapping of 20+ critical raw materials supply chains from industry databases (which is material cost and supply tracking in another sector), the VBA dashboard at Itaú that replaced a manual tracking process across locations, and the ensemble property valuation model in the top 2% of its competition. Python, Power BI, SQL and AI data analysis are all named in the posting and all evidenced more than once.

**The construction credibility underneath both:** developments carried past feasibility into design development, technical documentation and construction at Isay Weinfeld, the studio's first ground-up building followed from land-use analysis through construction at Felipe Hess, development cost tested against the buildable programme, and technical documentation reviewed as a compliance gate before construction was authorised at Itaú-Unibanco. He is not a data analyst who has never been on a project.

**Known gaps, not papered over:**

- **No construction cost database, and no cost benchmarking.** He has modelled development cost inside feasibility studies, not assembled and maintained a cost and programme dataset across a portfolio. This is the honest centre of the gap.
- **No RICS, and not currently working towards it.** The posting says "possess or working towards", so it is a soft requirement, and it is worth saying in interview that he would pursue it.
- **No preconstruction process experience** in the formal sense, and no quantity surveying background.
- **Degree is architecture, not engineering or construction.** The posting asks for "a relevant Engineering or Construction related field", and architecture plus a Master's Degree in Architecture Computation is adjacent rather than exact. This is the most likely screening risk.

**Why this one is worth real effort:** the gaps that kill his investment-analyst applications, no transaction experience and no DCF modelling, are not asked for here. What is asked for is data capability plus construction understanding, which is the combination he actually has, and the posting states no minimum project experience. The seat also writes the bridge to the investment teams into the job description itself, which makes it a genuine route inward.

**Practical flags:**

- **Location is not stated.** The role supports the European region with travel across Europe and exceptionally to Houston. Confirm the base city before sending: anywhere in the EU or EEA is clean on eligibility, and a London base would need Skilled Worker sponsorship.
- European languages are named as an advantage: Portuguese native, Spanish fluent, Danish and Italian at limited working proficiency.
- The Sweco anchor bullet still lists a lender as "IFDK", which is almost certainly a typo. It appears on this CV too and is still worth fixing at source.
