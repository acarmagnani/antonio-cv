Dear Hiring Team at Rystad Energy,

I trained as an architect, and what progressively drew me beyond the drawings were the questions a design studio rarely stops on - why something is being built, whether it should be built there at all, and what its impact will be over its life. Following those questions took me to an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy and, within it, specifically toward data, which is the part that makes it possible to answer them with evidence instead of intuition, and from there into sustainability and responsible business consulting. Rystad Energy asks those questions at the scale of entire markets rather than single assets, and an Advisory team that carries them from commercial due diligence for investors through to corporate strategy and policy design is where I want to work on them.

At Sweco I supported advisory teams delivering water, energy and transport projects financed by development banks including the World Bank, EBRD, KfW and EIB, and authored climate risk assessments for major water supply projects under JASPERS and EBRD climate-proofing guidance, screening hazards against projection data and rating exposure and vulnerability asset by asset. Working to lender standards taught me that an assessment is only useful if it survives review by someone with money at stake, which changes how you evidence every claim you make. Advising an investor on a transaction, or an energy company on its strategy, is held to that same standard, since the conclusion has to survive scrutiny from people whose capital depends on it.

During my time in Ramboll Management Consulting's Responsible Business Conduct team I ran human rights due diligence for corporate clients under the UN Guiding Principles and the OECD Guidelines, and in practice a large part of that was data work. I built a fully automated risk assessment system in Excel that consolidated supply chain risk data into a single dashboard which updated salient risks dynamically, an impact assessment tool that scored more than forty risks by severity so the most material ones surfaced first, and a mapping of the supply chains of over twenty critical raw materials under the EU Critical Raw Materials Act. The common task underneath all of it was taking broad and uneven bodies of evidence and structuring them rigorously enough to produce scored, defensible outputs. Quantitative and qualitative analysis of energy markets is that task again on a larger and more consequential body of evidence, and the tooling I built to move through it faster is the same instinct behind applying AI systems to research at volume.

Rystad Energy holds a position very few firms do, supplying the data and the independent reading of it that governments, investors and energy companies argue from as the energy transition unfolds. That is where I want to keep doing the work I find most worthwhile, which is turning large and awkward bodies of data into something a business can decide on.

Best,
Antonio Carmagnani
