Dear Hiring Team at CBRE Investment Management,

I trained as an architect in Brazil, and the pull was always toward the questions upstream of the drawing. What a building is worth, what it costs to keep standing, and what would make it work harder. That took me from design into feasibility and asset analysis, and eventually from São Paulo to Copenhagen for an M.Arch. in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, a programme about using data to make better decisions on real estate. You describe CBRE Investment Management as having an investor-operator culture, and that split happens to be the one I have been straddling for years.

The operator half is real. At Itaú-Unibanco I reviewed technical and regulatory documentation on the bank's property projects as a compliance gate before construction was authorised, and assessed the environmental and energy performance of buildings the bank was acquiring or leasing as part of internal due diligence. At Isay Weinfeld I carried developments past feasibility into design development, technical documentation and construction. Walking an office building or a shopping centre and knowing what I am looking at is not something I would need to learn.

The investor half came earlier and stayed. At OSPA I produced more than fifteen feasibility studies across Brazil and the United States, sizing investment and return against buildable area, unit mix and price per square metre. At Felipe Hess Architects I ran the financial feasibility against what each site could legally hold, and at Sweco I built a pricing tool parametrizing fee bands and project economics so bid decisions rested on something firmer than instinct.

At Ramboll I developed the firm's real estate ESG due diligence offering around building energy and decarbonisation under EU building performance regulation, and produced a supplier-facing human rights resource setting labour expectations for contractors on site. Your posting puts sustainability and social impact next to refurbishment and leasing rather than in a separate report, which is the only place that work does anything.

Urban Destinations is where the operating side of real estate shows up in the numbers, and that is the part I want to work on.

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani
