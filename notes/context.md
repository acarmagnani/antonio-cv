The three CV blocks in `presets.yaml` encode this: `real-estate-investment` is the one he is steering toward, `esg-built-environment` and `data-built-environment` are the adjacent tracks he still applies to while he gets there.

Two corrections to what this file used to say:

- **Green-certification (BREEAM/LEED/DGNB) is no longer a target direction.** It is the lowest-paying branch of everything he is considering, a technical compliance service with a low ceiling. For the sustainability-plus-finance hybrid, **GRESB, CRREM and EU Taxonomy Article 8 are worth more than a BREEAM assessor licence**, because those are the ones investors read.
- **The seat matters more than the subject.** An investment or asset management role with sustainability fluency pays roughly twice what a sustainability specialist inside the same firm does, because the seat carries deal responsibility. When both exist, aim for the investment side.

## Compensation (an explicit criterion, not a taboo)

Money is one of the reasons he left architecture, and it is a legitimate filter on a posting. He is explicit about it.

- **His benchmark** is his parents' standard of living in São Paulo: roughly R$200k/month combined, about R$100k each. That is top 0.1% in Brazil.
- **That level is an ownership, partner, or carry outcome, not a salary outcome.** Nobody reaches it on a payroll band. Practical consequence: when comparing two paths, ask which one leads to equity, carry, or a promote, not which has the higher starting salary.
- **This is why real estate investment beats ESG consulting for him.** Entry pay is similar; by year ten the investment path is roughly 1.5x to 2.5x, and the tail is far fatter because of bonus and carried interest. ESG consulting has neither.
- Nordic pay is compressed (high floor, low ceiling, around 56% marginal tax). London, Brazil, Switzerland and Dubai are where the numbers he named actually exist. Worth remembering when a posting's geography is otherwise neutral.

## Antonio's journey (the storytelling spine)

Antonio trained and worked as an architect in Brazil and is genuinely passionate about the built environment, real estate, and its financial and market side (why things get built, how the market moves). Alongside architecture he has two long-standing interests: data (using it to understand what is built and what could be built better) and environment / ESG / sustainability.

He was drawn away from architecture's execution focus (client wishes, design, delivery) toward the upstream, strategic questions it usually skips: why is this being built here, where and how could it be done better. To bring real estate, data, sustainability, and that strategic lens together, he moved from Brazil to Denmark for the M.Arch in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy, whose aim is to use data, technology, and innovation to improve real estate and
construction.
