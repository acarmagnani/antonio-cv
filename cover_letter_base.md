# Cover Letter — Base

This is the reference cover letter and voice anchor. To write a letter for a real job, Claude
reads this + that application's `job_description.md` + `content_base.yaml` (plus Antonio's
journey below), keeps this voice and four-paragraph architecture, leads with the relevant
strand of Antonio's journey, and swaps the experience paragraphs for the ones closest to the
role. Antonio edits this file to shape the default voice; it is the anchor every future letter
is written to match.

---

## The letter (template)

Dear Hiring Team at {Company},

{Grounded opening rooted in Antonio's journey: who he is and the interest that pulls him toward
this role, e.g. "I trained as an architect, but the part of the work that drew me most was the
step before the drawings, {the upstream question this role lives on, e.g. deciding what a
building is worth and what it could be}."} {One or two sentences carrying the journey strands
most relevant here, the built environment and the market, data, or sustainability, toward what
{Company} actually does.} {The role} at {Company}, where {what makes this company or team
distinctive}, is where I want to take that further.

During my experience as {the anchor role at the anchor employer}, I {two or three concrete
things I did there, in one flowing sentence, with real detail and numbers where possible}. {A
sentence abstracting that to the quality the role demands, e.g. reading an asset's constraints,
turning them into numbers, and landing on a decision people can act on.}

{One more paragraph carrying the other relevant strand, e.g. the data or architecture thread,
with a concrete achievement (a tool, a model, a number), tied back to the throughline without a
rhetorical flourish.}

{One grounded line on {Company}'s profile connected to where Antonio wants to go. End there, no
boilerplate ask.}

All the best,
Antonio Carmagnani

## Antonio's journey (the storytelling spine)

Every letter is built on Antonio's real career journey. Select the two or three strands most
relevant to the role and draw them in parallel to what the company does; the arc is what makes
the letter read as human rather than generic. The journey:

- Trained and worked as an architect in Brazil, genuinely passionate about the built
  environment, real estate, and its financial and market side (why things get built, how the
  market moves).
- Two long-standing interests running alongside architecture: data (using it to understand
  what is built and what could be built better) and environment / ESG / sustainability.
- Came to feel that architecture practice stops at execution (client wishes, design, delivery)
  and skips the upstream, strategic questions he cares about: why is this being built here,
  where and how could it be done better.
- To bring real estate, data, sustainability, and that strategic lens together, he moved from
  Brazil to Denmark for the M.Arch in Architecture Computation at The Royal Danish Academy,
  whose aim is to use data, technology, and innovation to improve real estate and construction.

Which strands to lead with: for a real-estate / proptech / valuation role, the built-environment
+ market + data strands; for an ESG / sustainability role, the sustainability + data strands.
Always anchored in the architect-who-moved-upstream arc. (Two examples of this done well: the EY
CCaSS and Clikalia letters.)

## How Claude tailors this per job

1. **Open on the journey.** Lead with Antonio's genuine arc (architect who moved upstream toward
   data, strategy, and sustainability), choosing the 2-3 strands that fit this role, tied to
   something specific about the company. The first line should be grounded (who he is), never
   abstract boilerplate, never "I'm excited to apply."
2. **Find the throughline** the journey points at for THIS role, the one idea every paragraph
   returns to (e.g. "making sustainability rigorous and data-driven"; "valuing residential
   assets with data"; "structuring complexity").
3. **Anchor paragraph:** the ONE experience from `content_base.yaml` that best fits. Concrete
   (real details, numbers), then abstracted to the quality the role demands.
4. **Supporting paragraph:** one or two more experiences with a concrete detail, carrying the
   second strand, tied back to the throughline.
5. **Close:** one grounded line on the company and where Antonio wants to go. End there. No
   "let's talk", no "I would welcome", no boilerplate ask.
6. ~300-350 words, one page. Name specific tools/methods the job asks for that Antonio genuinely
   has. One narrative for this role; do not re-narrate the CV.

## Style (keep it human, not AI)

- **Few or no colons.** The repeated "statement: reveal" structure is a dead AI tell. Use
  periods and commas instead.
- **No em dashes** (house rule). Commas, periods, parentheses.
- **Drop the flourishes.** Avoid ending on "X and Y are the same thing", or on "is exactly where
  I want to...". At most once per letter, ideally zero.
- **Vary** sentence length and openings; do not start consecutive sentences the same way.
- **Grounded opening**, not a casual "What I most want to do is...".
- **Language:** Antonio is fluent in Spanish. For roles in Spain, default the cover letter to
  Spanish (the CV stays in English) unless he says otherwise.

## Per-application notes from Antonio (optional, but gold)

If Antonio adds two or three raw sentences (why THIS company, a specific hook, a personal
connection), work them in. If a genuine, non-generic company hook is missing and cannot be
inferred, ask him rather than inventing enthusiasm.

## Banned (dead AI giveaways, never write these)

- "I would welcome the opportunity / possibility to..."
- "I am excited to apply" / "I am writing to express my interest"
- "aligns closely with" / "aligns with your mission"
- "I am confident I would be a great fit"
- ending on "Thank you for considering my application"
- any opener that could appear on any letter for any job
