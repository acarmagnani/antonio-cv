# Cover Letter — Base

This is the reference cover letter, written generic with {slots} for the job-specific
parts. To write a letter for a real job, Claude reads this + that application's
`job_description.md` + `content_base.yaml`, keeps this exact voice and four-paragraph
architecture, fills the slots, and swaps the experience paragraphs for the ones closest
to the role. Antonio edits the letter below to shape the default voice; it is the anchor
every future letter is written to match.

---

## The letter

Dear Hiring Team at {Company},

The work that has consistently engaged me most, across {the two or three of my fields
most relevant to this role, e.g. architecture practice, real estate development, and
infrastructure consulting}, is {the throughline this role is really about, e.g.
structuring complexity: coordinating technical inputs across disciplines and turning
them into something people can act on}. {The role} at {Company}, where {what makes this
company or team distinctive, e.g. engineering, design, and sustainability come together},
is where I want to take that further.

During my experience as {the anchor role at the anchor employer}, I {two or three
concrete things I did there, in one flowing sentence, with real detail}. The work
required {the underlying demands the role also needs, e.g. coordinating with multiple
stakeholders, synthesizing technical inputs, and producing structured outputs under
deadlines}. That combination of {two qualities, e.g. technical rigor and organizational
clarity} is what {this kind of work} demands, and it is what that role trained me to
deliver.

Since then, I have built on that foundation through {my other relevant experience}. At
{employer}, I {a concrete achievement with a specific detail or number}; more recently,
at {employer}, I {another concrete achievement}. Both reinforced what I first developed
at {the anchor employer}: that {the throughline restated, e.g. delivery quality comes
from combining technical understanding with structured coordination}.

{Company}'s profile as a firm that {what it is known for, connected to where I want to
go} is precisely the environment where I want to {my direction, e.g. develop a long-term
career in this field}.

Best regards,
Antonio Carmagnani

---

## How Claude tailors this per job

1. **Find the throughline first.** The one idea the role is really about, that every
   paragraph points back to. For a project-management role it was "structuring
   complexity"; for an ESG role it might be "making sustainability rigorous and
   data-driven"; for a proptech role, "turning built-environment data into decisions."
2. **Opening:** name the two or three of Antonio's fields most relevant to the role,
   state the throughline, then tie it to what makes this specific company/role
   distinctive (from the job description and Antonio's notes). Never "I'm excited to apply."
3. **Anchor paragraph:** pick the ONE experience from `content_base.yaml` that best fits
   the role. Describe it concretely (real details, numbers), then abstract it to the
   quality the role demands.
4. **Supporting paragraph:** one or two more experiences, each with a concrete detail,
   then a single synthesizing sentence that ties them back to the throughline.
5. **Close:** one sentence on the company's profile and where Antonio wants to go. End
   there. No "let's talk", no "I would welcome", no boilerplate ask.
6. ~300-350 words, one page. Name specific tools/methods the job asks for that Antonio
   genuinely has. Connect the experiences into one narrative for this role; do not just
   re-narrate the CV.

## Per-application notes from Antonio (optional, but this kills genericness)
Two or three raw sentences make the letter real, and Claude cannot invent them:
- Why THIS company (their projects, values, a personal connection, anything genuine).
- Any specific hook or throughline he wants to lead with.

## Banned (dead AI giveaways — never write these)
- "I would welcome the opportunity / possibility to..."
- "I am excited to apply" / "I am writing to express my interest"
- "aligns closely with" / "aligns with your mission"
- "I am confident I would be a great fit"
- ending on "Thank you for considering my application"
- any opener that could appear on any letter for any job
