## Who he is, and it is considerably more than I described

I called him my team leader. His title is **Head of International at Sweco**, since June 2025, and he runs the whole department: strategy, line management of more than fifteen staff, recruitment and growth, business development and acquisition, and P&L for a business line turning over more than five million euros a year.

Before Sweco he spent six and a half years at NIRAS International Consulting, as Team Manager from 2023 to 2025 running a team of ten, and before that as Project Manager and Consultant in Fund Management and Finance, managing a portfolio of contracts worth more than twelve million euros, overseeing funds of fifty-two million, and leading tender processes that won more than twenty-four million euros of new business.

**Why this matters.** This is the strongest signature of the three Sweco referees, and it carries authority on exactly what I need vouched for: whether someone can be handed something commercially or operationally important and be left alone with it. The letter uses his real title and lets the department, the headcount and the P&L do their work in the first paragraph.

## The umbrella

**Consulting and project delivery in the built environment and infrastructure, with the commercial side of it.** Concretely: consulting graduate and analyst seats, project management and technical due diligence in real estate and construction, business development and market analysis, and strategic project roles inside developers and asset owners. Emma takes the data and spatial half, Kirsten takes climate risk, and this letter stays out of both.

## What we actually did together

- **The Bangladesh branch office, which is the centrepiece.** Sweco was establishing a new branch in Bangladesh and I supported the operational side throughout: drafting employment contracts, handling salary and personnel data for each new hire, getting people onto the company's IT and email systems, and running the full health insurance onboarding with the provider, including going back to each new employee for the information the insurer required.
- **Running it to completion while he was away, which is the sentence that carries the letter.** He was out of the office for several weeks with the setup unfinished, the insurance package still open and a series of items outstanding. I closed all of it while simultaneously on the field mission across Baku's water supply system, and when he returned and asked what was left, there was nothing left. He said he had been able to come back without picking up a single item. **Phrasing:** it was parental leave, and the letter says "while I was away from the office for several weeks", which is accurate, discreet and standard.
- **The commercial work of the department**, which is his territory as its head: assembling bid teams for competitive international tenders by sourcing the specialist consultants each procurement required, writing technical and methodology sections of proposals, and building the pricing tool that parametrised expert fee bands and project economics so bid decisions rested on figures. He did not sit beside me for these, so the letter frames them as departmental work I contributed to, which he can vouch for as the person accountable for the department's results. **Do not call the pricing tool my own initiative**, which I never said and cannot support.
- **The mentoring and the curiosity behind it.** I asked him how the business actually works and he took the time to walk me through the financial and operational model of internationally financed infrastructure projects, after which I kept coming back with questions. He has said this curiosity is what stood out. It belongs in the closing paragraph, told as something he observed.

## His feedback

It overlaps heavily with Emma's, which is exactly why the two letters have to differ in their examples rather than in their adjectives.

- **Business development, proposals and tenders**, which European Energy states as shaping business development proposals and developing sales materials, Cadeler as trackers, reports and business development processes for senior management, and Capgemini as contributing to the growth of the offering. **What this letter must not claim:** financial modelling, valuation or waterfall structures, which Greystar asks for and I cannot support; GIS and cartography, which are Emma's; climate risk methodology, which is Kirsten's.

## Shape, and the opening in particular

Four paragraphs, one page.

1. **Opening, and it must differ from both Tereza's and Emma's.** Tereza's opens "I am writing in support of", and Emma's opens from her vantage as someone who held the role before. His should open from the department: who he is, what the International Department does and at what scale, and where Antonio sat in it, before stating the umbrella. Coming from the person accountable for the business line, the scale is not boasting, it is context that tells the reader what kind of environment the judgement was formed in.
2. **Bangladesh**, including the handover, told plainly and ending on what it meant for him.
3. **The commercial side**, bid teams, methodology and the pricing tool, framed as the department's work and what I contributed to it.
4. **Closing**, the curiosity and the mentoring, the technical ability colleagues remarked on, the internal feedback, and the recommendation naming the seats in the umbrella.
