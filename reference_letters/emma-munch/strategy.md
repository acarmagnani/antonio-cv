# Reference letter, Emma Munch (Sweco Danmark, International Department)

The shared rules (umbrella principle, letter shape, format, voice, truthfulness, process) live in `reference_letters/strategy.md` and are not repeated here.

## Status

Asked by email on 2026-09-08, after the goodbye at the Sweco office. She said yes and asked for a draft. Her LinkedIn export is in this folder.

The letter is written from this brief: `reference_letter.md` is the source and `reference_letter.docx` is what she receives. An earlier draft, written before this analysis, opened the way Tereza's does and reached for detail that answered no posting; it was replaced rather than patched.

## Who she is

- **Project Manager at Sweco Danmark A/S, International Department, Copenhagen**, since August 2025.
- **She held my job before I did**, as Project Assistant from January 2023 to August 2025, and Student Assistant before that. She has been in the department for close to four years and started three years before me. This is the most useful fact in her file: a referee who did the role herself and then moved up into managing it can say what it demands and whether someone cleared the bar, which is a stronger and more specific claim than praise from someone who only ever watched from above.
- Educated at the University of Copenhagen.
- She was senior to me and delegated work to me without being my line manager, so the letter is written from that vantage and never claims supervision she did not have.

## The umbrella

**Built environment data and analysis.** Concretely: real estate research and data analyst seats, GIS and spatial data roles, site and technical due diligence analysis, and construction and project data work. This is the half of my Sweco record that points at data, and it is deliberately the half that reaches back toward real estate, which is the destination. Lukas takes the commercial and project delivery side, Kirsten takes climate risk, and this letter stays out of both.

## What we actually did together, and the honest shape of it

We did not share a large project, and the letter must not pretend otherwise. What exists is repeated delegation, one piece of real initiative, and a clear reading of my work from inside the office. That is a good reference when written as what it is, and it is why this letter is shorter than Tereza's.

- **Cartography and geospatial work, repeatedly and by default.** Whenever a document needed a map, she came to me, and this happened many times: georeferenced project data for infrastructure in developing countries, cartographic material locating assets across several international projects, feeding methodology documents, inception reports and tender proposals. Behind it sits the QGIS work in `content_base.yaml`: geospatial databases compiling georeferenced asset data, satellite imagery and open sources. The claim the letter makes is not that I can operate QGIS, which is common, but that I became the person in the department this went to, and that what came back was ready for a client-facing document.
- **The SharePoint to OneDrive migration, which is the initiative story.** Started before I arrived and left half finished, several hundred folders, no record of which had already been moved so each had to be opened and checked by hand. Rather than continuing manually, which is what it had been and would have run for several more weeks, I scripted it: the routine worked through the folder tree, compared what had already been migrated against what had not, and ran overnight, finishing the whole thing by the next morning. **Do not name a language**, because I do not remember whether it was Python or shell. **Do not describe the failure modes**, the long file paths and the rest: that detail answers nothing any posting asks and reads as filler.
- Nothing else of substance, and the letter says so by not inventing a third project.

## Her feedback, which is the strongest material in this file

Given when I left: my communication is very good, in writing and in person; I show real enthusiasm for the work and for learning, and always seemed to want to take on more; she was happy with the speed and the quality; she had no doubt I was ready for a role beyond an internship, that I knew what I was doing, and that she could rely on me. She also said that when she asked colleagues across the office for their impressions, people had been surprised by how technically capable I was, in Excel and QGIS specifically and equally in the written assessment work, that everyone found it pleasant to work with me, and that colleagues were comfortable handing me a task without having to explain much, knowing it would come back done properly.

That last point is the spine of the closing paragraph, because it describes what it is like to work with someone rather than listing skills, and it is what a hiring manager is actually trying to find out.

## The postings this letter is written for, and their words

Read across the applications in this umbrella: Montagu Evans Research Analyst, Savills España Junior Data Analyst, Cushman & Wakefield Research and Reporting Analyst, Hines Construction Data Research Analyst, ADYD Data Centre Site Analyst, Sweco Digital GIS Consultant, NIRAS, GXN. What they ask, in their language, and what answers it:

- **"GIS (QGIS preferred) and spatial data analysis skills"**, which Montagu Evans lists as a requirement for a real estate research analyst, and which Sweco Digital states as ArcGIS or QGIS plus scripting and automation. This is the single most valuable sentence Emma can write, because it is a hard requirement in a target-family posting and she watched me deliver it repeatedly.
- **"Sourcing of relevant datasets and information"** and **"managing and interrogating third party datasets"** (Montagu Evans), and **"develop a database and repository for collected data"** (Hines). The QGIS databases compiled from asset data, satellite imagery and open sources are exactly this, and the letter should describe them as building a dataset rather than as making maps.
- **"Developing new ways to visualise data and explain trends"** (Montagu Evans) and **"desarrollo, mantenimiento y optimización de dashboards"** (Savills). Cartography that shows where infrastructure sits and what surrounds it is data visualisation applied to space, and framing it that way is honest and lands better than the word map.
- **"Automatización de procesos de reporting y tratamiento de datos"** (Savills), **"scripting/automatisering"** (Sweco Digital) and **"develop and implement tools"** (Hines). The migration is the evidence, and it should be told as automating a manual process that was consuming the team's time rather than as an IT chore.
- **"Contributing to pitches, presentations and client reports"** (Montagu Evans) and **"elaboración de informes con conclusiones y recomendaciones"** (ADYD). What I produced went into methodology documents, inception reports and tender proposals that went to clients and lenders, and saying where the work ended up is what turns a task into a contribution.
- **"Colaboración con distintos equipos para entender necesidades y traducirlas en soluciones"** (Savills). This is precisely the relationship she can describe: she came with a need, not a specification.
- **Excel**, named by every posting in this family, and named in the office feedback she collected.
- **Reliability and autonomy**, which no posting lists as a requirement and which decides junior hires more often than any listed requirement does. It is the closing paragraph.

**What this letter must not claim:** Power BI and SQL, which several of these postings ask for and which she never saw me use; financial modelling and valuation; climate risk methodology, which is Kirsten's; and the tender and commercial work, which is Lukas's.

## Shape, and the opening in particular

Four paragraphs, one page, shorter than Tereza's.

1. **Opening, and it must not begin the way Tereza's does.** Hers opens "I am writing in support of". Emma's should open from her own vantage instead, something to the effect that she worked with Antonio throughout his time in the department and had held the same role herself before moving into project management, which gives her a precise basis for comparison. The umbrella statement follows: the department's work on internationally financed water, energy and transport infrastructure, and the data and spatial side of it that she saw at first hand.
2. **The geospatial work**, told as dataset building and visual analysis that fed client-facing and proposal documents, with QGIS named.
3. **The migration**, told as automating a stalled manual process, with the outcome and without the technical trivia.
4. **Closing**, built on the office feedback: technical capability that surprised people, the written work, communication, appetite, and above all that work could be handed over without instructions and would come back done. Then the recommendation, naming the seats in the umbrella.
